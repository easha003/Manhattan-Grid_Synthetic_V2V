# Deep-reading notes: workbook entries W23–W24

These notes resolve the final two workbook rows. `W23` contains two different papers by the
same research group, so this dossier covers three works: `W23a`, `W23b`, and `W24`.

## Evidence convention

- **Direct** means reported in, or immediately implied by, a primary full text.
- **Calculated** means derived from a paper's reported numbers or figures.
- **Secondary reconstruction** means reported by later peer-reviewed work or an official
  university thesis when the original paper is closed.
- **Inference / transfer** means an engineering conclusion for the planned simulator, not a source
  claim.
- **Caveat** marks missing detail, contradiction, access limitation, or validity risk.

All links were checked on 2026-07-28. The complete publisher PDFs were read for both `W23`
papers. For `W24`, primary metadata and the author-posted abstract were verified, but no lawful
open full text was found. Its inaccessible method details are therefore kept visibly separate from
primary evidence.

## W23a — VLC-supported RL traffic control at two intersections

### Exact citation and access

Manuel Augusto Vieira, Gonçalo Galvão, Manuela Vieira, Paula Louro, Mário Vestias, and Pedro
Vieira, “Enhancing Urban Intersection Efficiency: Visible Light Communication and Learning-Based
Control for Traffic Signal Optimization and Vehicle Management,” *Symmetry*, vol. 16, no. 2,
article 240, 2024.

- DOI and open publisher article: <https://doi.org/10.3390/sym16020240>
- Publisher PDF: <https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/16/2/240/pdf>
- **Access depth:** complete 25-page CC BY publisher paper read, including communication
  protocol, optical signal illustrations, SUMO construction, RL description, and results.

### Research question and actual contribution

**Direct.** The paper combines two ideas:

1. a proposed V-VLC localization/message architecture among streetlights, traffic lights,
   connected vehicles, and pedestrians; and
2. learned signal-phase control for two adjacent urban intersections in SUMO.

The traffic controller uses vehicle distribution, position, velocity, queue, request, and response
information that the paper assumes can be supplied through V-VLC messages. Its objective is to
reduce accumulated waiting/queues and improve traffic flow.

This is a **traffic-signal-control paper**, not a hybrid-communications controller. Its learned
action selects a traffic-light phase; it never selects RF, VLC, duplication, MCS, power, or
communication resources.

### V-VLC architecture and message flow

**Direct.**

- Streetlights are placed about 20 m apart and serve as geo-referenced optical transmitters.
- Traffic signals and vehicle head/tail lamps participate in I2V, V2I, and indirect V2V message
  exchange; pedestrian devices participate in P2I/I2P exchange.
- A queue–request–response protocol assigns IDs and carries location, lane, direction, speed,
  route/request, following-vehicle, active-phase, waiting-time, and related traffic fields.
- Four-color red/green/blue/violet emitters produce multiplexed photocurrent levels. The paper
  illustrates calibrated multiplexing/demultiplexing and On–Off-Keying-style coded messages.
- Leading vehicles send intersection-crossing requests. Leader/follower pose and speed data are
  relayed to the intersection manager, which returns permission/phase information.

**Caveat.** The optical demonstrations establish a coding/localization concept, not a packet-level
vehicular channel:

- no RF fallback is simulated;
- no path-loss-to-BER/PER chain is coupled to SUMO;
- no random blockage, field-of-view loss, weather, ambient-light interference, packet loss, delay,
  retransmission, or feature staleness is applied to controller input; and
- the RL agent therefore receives traffic information as if the communication layer delivered it
  correctly.

### SUMO scenario

**Direct.**

- Two adjacent four-way intersections are modeled, each with two approaching and two departing
  lanes per arm.
- The original local arm description uses 100 m, while later RL tests describe inter-intersection
  road/arm lengths spanning roughly 160–400 m.
- Roads and flows are adapted from a Lisbon scenario.
- Vehicles nominally move at 10 m/s and slow to 5 m/s near the traffic-light/pedestrian
  transition.
- The high vehicle case corresponds to about 2300 vehicles/h; the lower case corresponds to about
  1800 vehicles/h.
- The broader dynamic simulation also describes 7200 pedestrians/h at one intersection and
  4000 pedestrians/h at the other, approximately 1 m/s walking speed.
- A one-hour episode contains 3600 one-second SUMO steps.

The approach to each intersection is divided into discrete response, request, and queue cells. One
description says five cells for each of eight lanes, or 40 state cells per intersection. The RL
section later says 32 cells per intersection. The neural network uses 80 inputs, which is consistent
with 40 cells across each of two intersections, but the manuscript never reconciles the 32/40-cell
descriptions.

### RL formulation

**Direct.**

- **Agent:** intersection manager / traffic-signal controller.
- **Observation:** discretized vehicle presence/distribution and velocities, derived from the
  assumed V-VLC information.
- **Action:** one of eight predefined vehicle traffic phases.
- **Timing:** an unchanged phase remains green for another 8 s; a changed action inserts a 4 s
  yellow phase before the new green.
- **Reward:** change in accumulated total vehicle waiting time. Increased waiting from `t-1` to
  `t` gives a negative reward; reduced waiting gives a positive reward.
- **Cooperation:** the two intersections are described as using an adjacent symmetric homogeneous
  reward so that both controllers account for flow on their connecting road.
- **Network:** 80 inputs, five fully connected hidden layers of 400 ReLU units, and eight Q-value
  outputs. Mean-squared error is stated for target-versus-predicted Q values.
- **Training:** 300 episodes; high/low tests use 3600 s per episode.

**Caveat.** Despite invoking Q values, the paper does not give a reproducible DQN specification:
discount factor, target-network update, replay capacity, batch size, optimizer/learning rate,
exploration schedule, terminal handling, normalization, random seeds, and checkpoint selection are
not reported.

### Evaluation and findings

**Direct.**

1. Figure 11 compares cumulative negative rewards when training with one versus two intersection
   agents. The two-agent curves appear to improve faster and oscillate less late in training.
2. The high- and low-traffic tests plot average speed and halted-vehicle count over one simulated
   hour. The high-load case reaches a roughly 30% larger halting peak and clears differently from
   the low-load case.
3. The reported evidence is primarily learning curves and descriptive time-series plots. It does
   not include confidence intervals, multiple independent training seeds, statistical tests, or a
   standard fixed-time/actuated/max-pressure/RL controller comparison on matched demand.

**Caveat.**

- The high/low-load plots mostly show expected traffic dynamics—speed falls as queues form and
  rises as the finite population clears—not an isolated causal advantage over a controller
  baseline.
- The conclusion says introducing and optimizing the pedestrian phase is the **next** focus,
  despite broader wording elsewhere about reducing pedestrian waiting. The trained controller
  should therefore not be cited as a completed joint vehicle/pedestrian RL result.
- “Real-world scenario” refers to a Lisbon-inspired simulation and communication proof-of-concept;
  the paper does not report a live intersection deployment.
- No communication reliability, latency, bandwidth, or error result is evaluated jointly with the
  learned policy.

### Relationship to the planned RF/VLC/DUP work

**Inference / transfer.**

Useful:

- SUMO/API coupling and one-second traffic-state extraction are examples of separating mobility
  simulation from an external controller.
- Discretized map-relative queue/request regions may inspire interpretable geometry features.
- Adjacent-agent coupling illustrates that an upstream action can create downstream congestion.

Not transferable as direct evidence:

- VLC is an assumed information source, not a stochastic service option.
- The controller acts every 8–12 s on signal phases, not per packet.
- There are no queues of communication packets, deadlines, reliability constraint, RF channel,
  duplication, or third-vehicle optical blocker.
- The 80×400×5 network and traffic reward are not justified starting hyperparameters for a compact
  packet controller.

## W23b — Five-intersection MARL–VLC traffic-signal control

### Exact citation and access

Manuel Augusto Vieira, Gonçalo Galvão, Manuela Vieira, Mário Véstias, Paula Louro, and Pedro
Vieira, “Decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Visible Light Communication for
Robust Urban Traffic Signal Control,” *Sustainability*, vol. 17, no. 22, article 10056, 2025.

- DOI and open publisher article: <https://doi.org/10.3390/su172210056>
- Publisher PDF: <https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/22/10056/pdf>
- **Access depth:** complete 32-page CC BY publisher paper read.

### Research question and contribution

**Direct.** This later work expands the group’s traffic-control setup to five homogeneous
intersections and includes:

- one local agent per intersection;
- shared experiences and a common DQN for homogeneous intersections;
- a neighbor-Q term intended to coordinate adjacent intersections;
- separate trained policies for directional-priority traffic strategies;
- explicit vehicle and pedestrian waiting in the reward; and
- Strategic Anti-Blocking Phase Adjustment (`SAPA`), a deterministic rule that lengthens green
  time using local queue and downstream occupancy.

Again, “MARL–VLC” means that traffic measurements and control messages are assumed to travel over
VLC. It does not mean the MARL policy controls a communication link.

### Network and V-VLC assumptions

**Direct.**

- Five four-arm intersections form two crossing arterials: horizontal `C0–C1–C2` and vertical
  `C3–C1–C4`, with `C1` as the central hub.
- `C1` has no exogenous source in the described setup; flows into it are released by the four
  neighboring intersections.
- Each approach has lanes for left and straight/right movements.
- Each controller chooses among nine phases: eight vehicle phases and one pedestrian phase.
- The V-VLC architecture uses streetlights as geo-transmitters and traffic signals as edge nodes.
  It assumes I2V/V2I and P2I/I2P messages supply position, velocity, trajectory, phase, request,
  queue, and waiting data.

The paper describes LED/OOK emitters, PIN photodetectors, multiplexed optical signals, and a
bit-field message format. Those descriptions do not become a channel/error model in the SUMO/RL
evaluation.

**Direct limitation from the conclusion.** The evaluation assumes ideal communications without
fully representing sensor or environmental uncertainty. Live deployment is listed as future work.

### MARL/DQN formulation

**Direct.**

- **Local observation:** `4 × 2 × 10 = 80` position cells, 80 velocity cells, and four waiting
  cells, for 164 inputs per intersection.
- **Action:** one of nine traffic phases.
- **Network:** five hidden layers, 400 ReLU units each, and nine Q outputs.
- **Learning components:** online prediction network, target network, shared replay experience,
  MSE loss, batch size 100, and learning rate 0.001.
- **Training table:** 200 episodes, 3600 maximum steps, 1800 vehicles, 2000 pedestrians, and
  “500 training epochs.”
- **Later experimental text:** 300 episodes of 3600 s. The 200-versus-300 discrepancy is not
  explained.

The target is written schematically as:

```text
Q_target =
    r_t + gamma * max_a' [
        Q_pred(s_{t+1}, a')
        + beta * average_neighbor_Q(n_{t+1}, a')
    ]
```

`beta=0.3` is chosen empirically. The reward is:

```text
r_t =
    p_vehicle * (accumulated_wait_vehicle(t-1) - accumulated_wait_vehicle(t))
  + p_pedestrian * (accumulated_wait_pedestrian(t-1) - accumulated_wait_pedestrian(t))
```

Waiting is accumulated while speed is below 0.1 m/s.

**Caveat.**

- The paper calls the system decentralized, but all homogeneous agents contribute experience to a
  unified network and centralized replay. Execution may be local; training/model ownership is
  shared rather than independent.
- `gamma`, the vehicle/pedestrian reward weights, target-update rule, replay capacity, exploration
  schedule, optimizer, synchronization frequency, seed protocol, and precise local-versus-shared
  network lifecycle are not reported.
- The prose calls the target network “not trained” but does not state how or when it tracks the
  online network.
- Adding predicted neighbor Q values is a heuristic coordination target, not a standard proof of
  decentralized optimality.

### Traffic strategies and SAPA

**Direct.** Five primary strategies bias demand/control between circular and radial arteries and
inbound/outbound directions:

1. balanced 50/50;
2. circular plus outbound radial;
3. circular plus inbound radial;
4. radial plus outbound radial; and
5. radial plus inbound radial.

Seventy-five percent of 1800 generated vehicles go straight/right and 25% turn left. In biased
cases, 65/35 splits and additional 75/25 directional splits generate the priority traffic.

The text later mentions “Networks 6–9” despite defining five networks in Table 3, another
unresolved presentation inconsistency.

`SAPA` augments the learned phase choice with a queue/occupancy rule:

- base green time is 8 s;
- on a 400 m link, green may be extended when downstream occupancy is below 40%;
- on a 200 m link, the threshold becomes 35%;
- low-priority movements receive a 25% weight; and
- if occupancy is high, the base duration is retained to reduce spillback.

The printed extension is proportional to `T_base × queue × artery_weight`. The paper does not
clearly specify queue normalization, a maximum-green cap, safety clearance constraints, or how
the formula is tuned outside the studied topology.

### Evaluation and findings

**Direct.**

- Traffic is 1800 vehicles/h and 2000 pedestrians/h in the main experiments.
- Separate networks are trained for different directional demand strategies.
- Reward curves suggest convergence but are not supported by formal statistical analysis; the
  authors explicitly describe the comparisons as simulation trends.
- The balanced strategy creates the greatest difficulty at central intersection `C1`; directional
  priority can reduce accumulation for its favored pattern.
- Network/strategy 4 shows the most favorable reported trends for its radial-heavy case.
- Comparing `beta=0` with `beta=0.3`, the latter produces fewer halted vehicles and pedestrians,
  especially at `C1`, and clears more participants in the simulated hour.
- In the balanced case, SAPA keeps the central queue around 20 vehicles with brief peaks near 40,
  while the fixed-8-s comparison frequently exceeds 40 and continues to accumulate.
- SAPA increases use of the pedestrian phase by more than 20% relative to the fixed-duration
  comparison in the reported phase-share plot.

### Validity limitations

1. The abstract claims validation with “preliminary real-world experiments,” but the methods,
   results, conclusion, and future-work statements document simulation and put real urban
   deployment in the future. No live-intersection experiment, sample, hardware deployment, or
   field performance table is presented.
2. Communication is ideal in the control experiment. There is no VLC loss, latency, blockage,
   ambient/weather process, optical interference, RF fallback, or sensor error.
3. The paper infers energy/emissions/sustainability benefits from reduced stopping. It does not use
   a fuel, power, or emissions model to quantify those claims.
4. Baselines are traffic strategies, `beta` ablations, and fixed-versus-SAPA duration. There is no
   matched fixed-time, actuated, max-pressure, independent-DQN, established MARL, or centralized
   controller suite.
5. Results are mostly single plotted trajectories/learning curves without confidence intervals,
   independent training seeds, hypothesis tests, or distribution-shift trials.
6. The topology contains only five homogeneous standard intersections. Claims about large,
   heterogeneous networks, roundabouts, robustness, and scalability are conceptual extrapolations.
7. The 200/300 episode conflict, five-versus-nine network wording, and missing RL hyperparameters
   prevent exact reproduction.

### Relationship to the planned RF/VLC/DUP work

**Inference / transfer.**

Potentially useful:

- explicit feature construction from position, velocity, queue, and waiting cells;
- shared experience/parameterization for homogeneous agents;
- representing downstream occupancy to prevent an upstream decision from causing spillback;
- separate ablations for communication/coordination strength and a fast deterministic safety
  layer around a learned action.

Do not transfer:

- treating “VLC data available” as evidence that the optical link is reliable;
- using neighbor Q predictions as a packet-level reliability guarantee;
- treating one simulated hour or a smooth reward curve as robust control evidence;
- copying the large five-by-400 MLP without dimension/sample/latency justification; or
- citing W23b as prior RF/VLC link-selection, duplication, URLLC, or constrained-RL work.

## W24 — AHP/cooperative-game vertical handover in indoor VLC–Femto

### Exact citation and primary access

Shufei Liang, Yuexia Zhang, Bo Fan, and Hui Tian, “Multi-Attribute Vertical Handover
Decision-Making Algorithm in a Hybrid VLC-Femto System,” *IEEE Communications Letters*,
vol. 21, no. 7, pp. 1521–1524, July 2017.

- DOI/IEEE record: <https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2017.2654252>
- DBLP record: <https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icl/LiangZFT17>
- Author-publication page carrying the abstract:
  <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312558040_Multi-Attribute_Vertical_Handover_Decision-Making_Algorithm_in_a_Hybrid_VLC-Femto_System>
- **Access depth:** primary title, authors, venue, pages, DOI, and abstract verified. IEEE full text
  was closed; ResearchGate provided “request full text” rather than a manuscript; OpenAlex,
  Semantic Scholar, and DBLP did not identify an open copy.

### What the primary abstract establishes

**Direct from the primary abstract.**

- Hybrid VLC and femtocell RF are used for complementary speed and connectivity.
- Vertical handover is formulated as **decision selection**.
- Analytic hierarchy process (`AHP`) incorporates multiple network and traffic attributes.
- A two-person cooperative game (`CG`) is added to address classic AHP's weakness in comparing
  actual criterion values.
- A rationality degree (`RD`) is computed for each decision from the AHP and CG results; the
  decision with the larger RD is selected.
- Simulations are said to outperform existing schemes.

That is the maximum defensible primary-source detail available without the closed four-page body.

### Method reconstructed from independent technical descriptions

Everything in this subsection is **secondary reconstruction**, not direct full-paper evidence.
Consistent later peer-reviewed descriptions and an official university thesis report:

- an indoor family-apartment system with eight VLC access points and one femtocell base station;
- two alternatives rather than a conventional ranking over many candidate networks:
  `perform VHO` and `do not perform VHO`;
- VLC-to-femto evaluation when VLC is inaccessible, blocked, overloaded, or offers inadequate
  service;
- femto-to-VLC evaluation when usable high-rate optical service returns;
- AHP weights that depend on traffic/application preference;
- the two decisions treated as the two “players” in a cooperative game, with their actual
  criterion values/average marginal contributions used to counter AHP's purely subjective
  weighting; and
- an AHP/CG combination producing the rationality degree used for the final binary choice.

Secondary descriptions associate the comparison with attributes such as:

- delay;
- queue length;
- data rate;
- traffic preference/type;
- average duration of VLC interruption; and
- aggregate/total VLC bit rate.

The set should be cited as a reconstructed description, because the unavailable paper body is
needed to verify the exact hierarchy and whether every listed metric is used in every direction and
traffic class.

### Reported result and its evidence tier

The primary abstract only says the AHP-CG algorithm is superior to existing schemes.

A later peer-reviewed handover paper reports that AHP-CG reduces “average vertical handover” by up
to 18% relative to AHP. That source does not make completely clear whether the quantity is a count,
rate, or another averaged handover measure. Therefore:

- **safe:** “a later paper reports up to 18% improvement in its average-VHO measure over AHP”;
- **unsafe:** an unqualified “W24 cuts handovers by 18%,” especially if interpreted as a universal
  vehicular result.

### What remains unverifiable

Without the primary body, the following must not be invented or silently filled with generic AHP
or Shapley-value formulas:

- exact AHP hierarchy and pairwise-comparison matrices;
- numerical weights and consistency ratios;
- cooperative-game characteristic function and marginal-contribution equation;
- rationality-degree equation and normalization;
- exact traffic classes and attributes per class;
- mobility, blockage, arrival, and channel distributions;
- simulation duration, replications, and random seeds;
- complete baseline set and quantitative curves; and
- statistical significance.

### Relationship to the planned RF/VLC/DUP work

**Inference / transfer.**

W24 is useful as a deterministic multi-criteria handover baseline:

1. normalize only measurable pre-action link/traffic features;
2. declare traffic-dependent weights;
3. compare `switch` and `stay`, or compare feasible `{RF,VLC,DUP}` actions;
4. add hysteresis/minimum dwell time; and
5. tune weights on validation traces, never on test outcomes.

However, an implementation should be labeled **W24-inspired** unless the exact primary equations
are obtained. Generic AHP may be implemented transparently, but it must not be called an exact
reproduction of the inaccessible AHP-CG algorithm.

W24 is:

- indoor rather than vehicular;
- VLC–femtocell rather than V2V/NR sidelink;
- handover rather than packet duplication;
- deterministic multi-attribute/game-theoretic control rather than RL; and
- not a reliability-constrained queue/deadline formulation.

## Cross-entry synthesis

### What W23 and W24 actually add

| Entry | Decision | Environment | Communication treatment | Reusable role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W23a | one of eight signal phases | two SUMO intersections | VLC supplies ideal traffic state | mobility/API example and cooperative traffic-state features |
| W23b | one of nine signal phases plus SAPA duration | five SUMO intersections | VLC supplies ideal local/neighbor state | shared-agent coordination and downstream-occupancy example |
| W24 | perform/do-not-perform vertical handover | indoor apartment VLC–Femto | network attributes enter AHP/CG | deterministic multi-attribute baseline |

None is a prior implementation of per-packet `{RF,VLC,DUP}` selection.

### Corrections to carry into the workbook

1. `W23` contains two separate articles from 2024 and 2025.
2. Both `W23` papers control **traffic lights**, not the RF/VLC communication mode.
3. W23a's pedestrian-aware control claims exceed the completed RL experiment described in its
   conclusion.
4. W23b's abstract says “preliminary real-world experiments,” but its own body/conclusion presents
   simulation and identifies real deployment as future work.
5. W23b assumes ideal communications and does not validate low-latency/high-reliability VLC in
   the learned loop.
6. W24 is AHP plus a cooperative game, not TOPSIS and not RL.
7. W24's detailed method and 18% comparison are secondary-reconstructed; they must not be labeled
   direct evidence.

### Coding implications

**Inference / transfer.**

- Keep SUMO/mobility state separate from the communications controller. Traffic-light phase
  control is outside the initial RF/VLC/DUP action.
- If geometry/kinematics arrive through sensing or messages, model their sampling delay, age,
  noise, and loss; do not inherit W23's perfect-VLC assumption.
- Use downstream load/queue information only if a deployed controller could receive it causally.
- Include a simple transparent weighted/AHP-style selector among non-learning baselines, while
  labeling it W24-inspired.
- Validate any coordination or belief feature under communication impairment and held-out
  topology, not only under a single homogeneous map.
