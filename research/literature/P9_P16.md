# Full-paper research notes: workbook entries P9–P16

These notes were prepared from the full texts, not from the short descriptions in
`RF_VLC_RL_literature_review.xlsx`. They are intended as a durable technical reference for the
planned Manhattan-grid NR-sidelink + vehicle-to-vehicle visible-light communication (V-VLC)
simulator and its constrained-RL controller.

## Evidence convention

- **Direct** means that the statement is reported by, or follows immediately from an equation or
  table in, the cited paper.
- **Calculated** means a simple calculation from reported numbers; it is not a number printed by
  the authors.
- **Inference / transfer** means an engineering conclusion for the planned simulator. It is not a
  claim made by the source paper.
- **Caveat** records missing detail, an inconsistency, or a limitation that matters when reusing the
  work.

The access date for all URLs below was 2026-07-28. The arXiv DOI attached to a preprint is a
DataCite identifier for the arXiv record; it must not be mistaken for a peer-reviewed journal or
conference DOI.

## Fast bibliographic corrections to the workbook

1. **P9** is not titled “Multi-Agent DRL Packet Duplication (Nokia).” Its exact title is
   *Hierarchical Multi-Objective Deep Reinforcement Learning for Packet Duplication in
   Multi-Connectivity for URLLC*.
2. **P11** uses the normal approximation without the workbook's `+ 0.5 log2(n)` correction term.
   That term may be useful in another finite-blocklength approximation, but it is not the equation
   used in P11.
3. **P12** is currently arXiv v3, revised 2026-01-28. Results and implementation details below
   therefore refer to v3, not necessarily the version originally entered in the spreadsheet.
4. **P14** is an arXiv technical report, not a journal or conference article.
5. **P15** does not establish that “surgery” itself makes a fixed task five times cheaper. The
   150 versus 770 PFLOP/s-day comparison is between a clean, final-environment rerun and the
   evolving ten-month research run. Surgery enabled continual iteration while the task and model
   changed; the clean rerun was both cheaper and ultimately stronger.
6. **P16** is now arXiv v2 (2026-04-10) and its record says it was accepted for the 2026 European
   Control Conference.

## P9 — Hierarchical multi-objective DRL for packet duplication

### Exact citation and access

Qiyang Zhao, Stefano Paris, Teemu Veijalainen, and Samad Ali, “Hierarchical Multi-Objective Deep
Reinforcement Learning for Packet Duplication in Multi-Connectivity for URLLC,” in *2021 Joint
European Conference on Networks and Communications & 6G Summit (EuCNC/6G Summit)*, Porto,
Portugal, 8–11 June 2021, pp. 142–147.

- Peer-reviewed conference DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1109/EuCNC/6GSummit51104.2021.9482453>
- University of Oulu repository record:
  <https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/handle/10024/29695>
- Repository PDF:
  <https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/bitstream/10024/29695/1/nbnfi-fe2021102151924.pdf>
- **Access depth:** all six pages of the repository full text were read.
- **Rights:** the repository marks the item In Copyright; it is not a CC-licensed work.

### Problem and contribution

**Direct.** 3GPP packet duplication can send the same PDCP packet over multiple
multi-connectivity legs. Spatial diversity can lower packet error and first-arrival latency, but
redundant transmissions consume spectrum, fill queues, and create interference for other UEs.
Consequently, “duplicate everything” can be worse than selecting only the packets and legs that
are likely to benefit.

The paper proposes:

1. a packet-level duplication decision at the PDCP layer;
2. a sequential actor–critic model using an LSTM to incorporate recent radio/traffic history;
3. a semi-centralized multi-agent training arrangement that combines simultaneous UEs' outcomes;
4. a product reward that maps reliability and delay to service-specific satisfaction scores.

The desired policy is network-aware: it should duplicate for a UE when a second leg has useful
signal quality and less queued work, but refrain when the second transmission mainly contributes
load or interference.

### Cellular system model

**Direct.**

- The setting is a heterogeneous cellular network with macro and small-cell layers.
- A dual-connected UE selects as its PCell the cell with strongest RSRP. The master gNB hosts
  SDAP and PDCP; an Xn link carries duplicated PDCP packets to a secondary gNB and its SCells.
- Release-15 is described as permitting as many as four copies: two component carriers at each of
  two gNBs.
- A cell-range-extension rule and a 10 dB dual-connectivity range first decide whether a UE is
  eligible for duplication. RL then selects the usable secondary leg combination.
- The measured PDCP delay includes both queueing and physical transmission time.
- The paper treats completion latency as the minimum across selected legs. It uses a
  product-form, independence-style expression for the combined transmission outcome.

**Caveat.** The mathematical fonts in the repository's searchable PDF are damaged. The prose calls
the combined quantity a “joint error probability,” while the extracted equation does not preserve
enough symbols to unambiguously distinguish a product of failure probabilities from one minus a
product of success probabilities. This is not a safe equation to transcribe blindly. For an
implementation, define events explicitly: duplicated delivery succeeds by the deadline if at least
one selected leg succeeds by the deadline.

The per-leg rate/delay expression includes transmit power, channel/antenna gain, thermal noise, and
an efficiency determined by the link-adaptation MCS.

### RL formulation

**Direct.**

- **State:** for each candidate cell/leg, RSRP at the UE, buffered traffic in the cell, gNB
  location, and antenna direction. The LSTM receives the current state plus the states of `K` past
  packets.
- **Action:** a stochastic duplication choice for the UE/packet, represented by the actor's
  probability of enabling or disabling available secondary legs.
- **Outcome:** delivery delay and an error statistic are returned after the receiver acknowledges
  delivery or loss.
- **Critic:** estimates the long-run value of an individual UE action using rewards pooled from
  UEs transmitting in the same TTI.
- **Actor update:** uses a TD-like difference between observed UE reward and critic value in a
  policy-gradient update.
- **Deployment:** batches of `(state, action, reward)` are periodically collected across gNBs to
  train the critic. TD values are returned to gNBs for actor training. Once trained, the local actor
  can choose duplication without per-packet network communication.

The paper calls the design hierarchical, but it is not a modern centralized-training/
decentralized-execution algorithm with an explicitly specified joint critic architecture. It is a
short high-level description of a network-aggregated critic plus local actor decisions.

### Reward and treatment of URLLC

**Direct.** Reliability and delay are mapped separately to bounded satisfaction rewards using
piecewise negative-log functions:

- the reliability reward is one once error is at or below the configured target and decays when it
  is worse;
- the delay reward is one below a lower target, decays between that target and an upper delay
  bound, and is zero beyond the upper bound;
- the final reward is the product of the two component rewards.

The authors' intent is to stop supplying extra incentive once a target is met and to prioritize
packets that can move toward simultaneous delay and reliability satisfaction.

The evaluation varies lower delay targets of 0.5 ms and 10 ms and fixes the upper delay cutoff at
50 ms. The PDF reports reliability-shaping weights of 0.1 and 0.2. From the paper's continuity
relationship `e_target = exp(-1 / w_e)`, these correspond to approximately `4.54e-5` and
`6.74e-3`, respectively.

**Caveat.** Superscripts in the printed target labels are corrupted in every text extraction
available from the repository, so the rounded base-10 exponents in the figure captions cannot be
quoted with confidence. The weights and the equation-derived numerical values above are
recoverable. The paper's introduction names the broad six-nines/1 ms aspiration, but its reward is
soft target shaping; it does not enforce a reliability constraint or prove a URLLC guarantee.

### Experimental design

**Direct.**

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| Macro layer | 7 gNBs, 3 sectors per gNB |
| Small-cell layer | 4 small cells per macro sector, 84 total |
| UEs | 288, uniformly placed; positions rerandomized each drop |
| Carrier frequency | macro 2 GHz; small cells 3.5 GHz |
| Bandwidth | 10 MHz per layer |
| Cell selection | 15 dB CRE, 10 dB dual-connectivity range |
| Traffic | FTP-like downlink, 100 packets/s/UE |
| Packet-size sweep | body says 25–600 bytes |
| Duration | 21,000 TTIs for each of 10 random drops |
| Heights | macro 32 m, small cell 10 m, UE 1.5 m |
| Transmit powers | macro 46 dBm, small cell 30 dBm |
| Propagation | urban macro and urban micro |
| SCS | 15 kHz |

Baselines are no duplication and standard/full duplication for every eligible UE. Curves examine
PER distribution, delay distribution, joint target satisfaction, mean delay under offered-load
sweeps, delivered-packet throughput, and allocated resources.

### Quantitative and qualitative results

**Direct.**

- Selective DRL has lower PER than no duplication, as expected from diversity, but is also reported
  to have lower PER than full duplication because it avoids transmissions on poor legs that create
  interference.
- For packets in the tail above 10 ms, the 0.5 ms reward target approximately halves the tail
  relative to the looser 10 ms target and is reported as about ten times lower than both no and full
  duplication.
- The strict reward setting yields better joint target satisfaction than full duplication. The
  relaxed setting achieves roughly similar satisfaction to full duplication.
- Above an offered load labelled about 40 MB/s/UE, DRL sharply lowers mean delay relative to the
  two fixed policies; full duplication overloads secondary-cell queues.
- Full-duplication throughput begins dropping above an offered load labelled about 20 MB/s/UE,
  whereas selective DRL limits the collapse.
- The stricter target increases duplication probability by about ten percentage points relative to
  the relaxed target.
- Selective DRL uses roughly 25–33% fewer resources for duplicated traffic than full duplication
  while improving the reported QoS-satisfaction curves.

**Caveat.** Most results are plotted CDFs or curves without numerical tables, confidence intervals,
or seed-level dispersion. The percentage figures above should not be given more precision than the
figures support.

### Assumptions and limitations

- **Direct/caveat:** the table says 25–600 **Kbytes**, while the body says 25–600 **bytes**. At
  600 bytes and 100 packets/s, the source rate is 60,000 bytes/s, not the paper's later “60 MB/s”
  label. Packet-size and load units are internally inconsistent.
- Static random drops replace continuous mobility trajectories. No vehicle kinematics, buildings,
  blockage, weather, VLC geometry, or V2V sidelink is modeled.
- Details required for reproduction are absent: LSTM history `K`, layer sizes, optimizer settings,
  learning rates, discount, training length separated from evaluation, seeds, and uncertainty.
- The multi-leg independence-style outcome model does not expose shared obstruction, common
  interference, or correlated queueing.
- “Target satisfaction” is an empirical soft-reward result, not a constrained-MDP guarantee.
- No explicit finite-blocklength coding model or per-packet 1 ms deadline test is used.

### Concrete transfer to the planned RF/VLC/DUP simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. Keep P9's central systems lesson: DUP must have an externality. Sending RF and VLC copies
   consumes both links' resources and may increase RF channel busy ratio/interference or queueing.
   A simulator in which DUP is a free `min(delay_RF, delay_VLC)` operation will structurally favor
   duplication and miss the paper's main result.
2. Implement completion semantics explicitly:
   `success_DUP = success_RF_by_deadline OR success_VLC_by_deadline` and
   `latency_DUP = min(successful arrival times)`. Model common-mode failure separately so the two
   legs are not automatically independent.
3. Use P9's product satisfaction reward only as a **soft-reward ablation**. The main controller
   should keep reward/resource performance separate from deadline-miss cost and update a
   Lagrange multiplier for the constraint.
4. Observable RF/VLC quality, recent link outcomes, and load are justified state features. Replace
   RSRP-only cellular context with RF SINR/CBR/queue plus VLC received optical power or SNR,
   incidence angle, line-of-sight/occlusion state, and feature age.
5. If the simulator grows from one transmitter–receiver pair to interacting pairs, a networkwide
   critic with local execution is a plausible second-stage design. It is unnecessary complexity for
   the first single-pair reference implementation.

## P10 — Dynamic packet duplication for industrial URLLC

### Exact citation and access

David Segura, Emil J. Khatib, and Raquel Barco, “Dynamic Packet Duplication for Industrial
URLLC,” *Sensors*, vol. 22, no. 2, article 587, 2022.

- Journal DOI: <https://doi.org/10.3390/s22020587>
- Publisher page: <https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/2/587>
- PubMed Central: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8777940/>
- Europe PMC structured full text:
  <https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC8777940/fullTextXML>
- PMID: 35062548
- **Access depth:** complete open-access JATS full text, including equations, tables, and
  references, was read.
- **License:** CC BY.

### Problem and contribution

**Direct.** Always-on NR dual-connectivity duplication improves the best-case latency CDF but
wastes capacity when the primary leg was already going to meet its deadline. The paper proposes a
supervised random-forest gate at the master node: predict the next packet's latency from recently
observed radio indicators and duplicate only when predicted latency exceeds 2 ms.

This is important for comparison design: it is **not reinforcement learning**. It is a lightweight
one-step supervised predictor feeding a threshold rule.

### Protocol and system details

**Direct.**

- Downlink NR–NR dual connectivity with a master node and a secondary node linked over Xn.
- PDCP gives both copies the same sequence number. RLC, MAC, PHY, beam selection, MCS,
  acknowledgements, and HARQ operate independently on each leg.
- The receiver passes up the first successfully decoded copy and discards a later copy.
- The latency estimator is trained offline at the network edge and runs at the master node.
- Features for the current duplication decision come from the immediately preceding packet:
  SINR, modulation index, and whether reception succeeded, including HARQ feedback.
- Decision rule: duplicate when the predicted latency is greater than 2 ms.

No state/action/reward tuple exists beyond that classification-and-gating pipeline.

### Simulation configuration

**Direct.**

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| Simulator | ns-3 with 5G-LENA |
| Facility | factory floor, 4,800 m² and 10 m height |
| Propagation | 3GPP TR 38.901 indoor-factory dense-high (InF-DH) |
| Clutter | 6 m height, 80% density |
| gNBs | 2 picocells, 8 m high, 50 m apart |
| Carrier / bandwidth | 3.7 GHz / 20 MHz |
| Antennas / power | omnidirectional / 23 dBm |
| Background users | 15 fixed/random UEs per gNB; 1 Mb/s CBR downlink |
| URLLC user | an AGV moving at 2 m/s; heading redrawn uniformly every 10 s |
| URLLC traffic | UDP, 64-byte packet every 10 ms |
| Numerology | 2; 0.25 ms slot |
| Scheduling | round-robin |
| Link adaptation | adaptive MCS, table 1 up to 64-QAM, target BLER |
| HARQ | at most one retransmission |
| Xn latency | 100 µs |
| MAC–PHY delay | 2 slots |
| Decode delays | data 100 µs; DCI 0 slots; feedback 1 slot |
| Compute placement | MEC is assumed, so RAN dominates end-to-end latency |
| Dataset size | 59,940 packet observations |

The paper discusses generic random-forest design choices but does not report the selected number
of trees, tree depth, feature sampling, split method, or random seed.

### Results

**Direct.**

| Metric | Reported value |
|---|---:|
| False-positive rate for `latency > 2 ms` | 0.0041% |
| False-negative rate | 0.0615% |
| Prediction accuracy | 99.9849% |
| Packets below 2 ms, single link | 81.6549% |
| Packets below 2 ms, always duplicate | 95.7891% |
| Packets below 2 ms, RF-gated duplicate | 95.7541% |
| Duplicated packets, always duplicate | 59,940 |
| Duplicated packets, gated | 11,376 |
| Reduction in duplicated packets | 81.0211% |
| Fraction of duplicated cases yielding latency reduction, always duplicate | 25.0917% |
| Fraction yielding latency reduction, gated | 86.5506% |

At a 1 ms threshold, approximately 55% of single-link packets, 62% of gated packets, and 70% of
always-duplicated packets arrive in time. The gated and always-on CDFs become almost the same by
about 1.75 ms. Conditional on triggering the gate, the authors report about a 75% probability of
reducing latency by more than 1 ms.

### Reliability, latency, and URLLC interpretation

**Direct.** Reliability is operationalized as the empirical probability of meeting a latency
threshold. The chosen trigger/evaluation threshold is 2 ms, not a formal 1 ms URLLC constraint.
The best result is about 95.79% under 2 ms, far from a `1 - 10^-5` or six-nines reliability target.
The paper therefore demonstrates efficient dynamic duplication, not canonical URLLC reliability.

### Assumptions and limitations

- The predictor assumes that successive packets retain relevant channel information. The paper
  writes a condition equivalent to `packet interval > coherence time`, estimates coherence time at
  about 20 ms, and then uses a 10 ms packet interval to argue that channels are similar. The
  inequality should logically be the opposite. This is an internal text error.
- A fixed 2 ms threshold is hand-selected rather than learned or constrained.
- There is no online adaptation, distribution-shift detector, uncertainty calibration, or retraining
  schedule; changing geometry and environmental conditions are left to future work.
- Two radio legs in one factory can share blockage and interference. Independence is more
  optimistic than a common-cause model.
- There is no confidence interval, train/test split description, seed protocol, or forest
  hyperparameter table.
- Only downlink cellular traffic is tested; there is no V2V sidelink, VLC, weather, Manhattan
  mobility, or coupled RF resource cost.
- Redundancy is counted, but it is not optimized in a CMDP and no deadline-miss budget is
  enforced.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. Implement this as a serious low-compute baseline:
   `DUP if estimated P(single-link deadline miss | pre-action features) > threshold`.
   A moving-window logistic model, random forest, or calibrated lookup table is acceptable, but
   every input must be available before the action.
2. Store lagged ACK, link-quality, and feature-age fields in traces. Do not let the gate see the
   current packet's realized error or latency; that would be counterfactual leakage.
3. Because simulation exposes counterfactual outcomes for all three actions, report not just DUP
   rate but “useful duplication” precision/recall: how often DUP prevented a miss and how often it
   was unnecessary.
4. First-arrival and duplicate-discard behavior should be common to this baseline and the RL
   environment.
5. Treat P10's 2 ms result as a separate reproduction setting. The planned 1 ms/rare-miss
   experiment must use its own target and much larger evaluation sample.

## P11 — Dynamic link adaptation for ultra-reliable 6G in-X subnetworks

### Exact citation and access

Fateme Salehi, Aamir Mahmood, Sarder Fakhrul Abedin, Kyi Thar, and Mikael Gidlund, “Towards
Ultra-Reliable 6G in-X Subnetworks: Dynamic Link Adaptation by Deep Reinforcement Learning,”
arXiv:2507.12031v1 [eess.SY], submitted 16 July 2025.

- arXiv record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12031>
- PDF: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.12031>
- arXiv/DataCite DOI: <https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.12031>
- **Access depth:** complete preprint and parameter tables were read.
- **Publication status:** no peer-reviewed venue was identified; treat it as a preprint.

### Problem and contribution

**Direct.** The paper considers a desired short-packet downlink surrounded by independent,
uncoordinated in-X subnetworks sharing unlicensed spectrum. It jointly adapts transmit power and
blocklength to reduce both bursts of reliability failures and transmission energy. It compares SAC,
DDPG, TD3, A2C, PPO, tabular Q-learning, random allocation, and maximum resources.

The primary contribution is framing *consecutive* reliability outages, rather than just average
error probability, as an objective alongside energy efficiency and availability.

### Physical and traffic model

**Direct.**

- Time is divided into 0.1 ms minislots.
- The desired subnetwork uses orthogonal OFDM subcarriers internally; interference comes from
  `N` external subnetworks sharing the band.
- Interferer activity is represented by Bernoulli indicators driven by activation factor `mu`; an
  active message persists for `l` minislots.
- Desired and interfering channels are single-antenna Rayleigh block fading.
- The instantaneous interference is a sum of active interferer powers times channel gains, and the
  desired SINR is desired received power divided by thermal noise plus that interference.
- A fresh `b`-bit message is sent without retransmission, motivated by freshness of industrial
  control information.

For blocklength `m`, power `p`, and instantaneous SINR `rho`, the paper uses:

```text
C(rho) = log2(1 + rho)
V(rho) = (log2(e))^2 * (1 - (1 + rho)^(-2))
epsilon ≈ Q(((C(rho) - b/m) * ln(2)) / sqrt(V(rho)/m))
```

Equivalently, the Q-function argument is `(m C - b) ln(2) / sqrt(m V)`.

**Correction.** There is no `+ 0.5 log2(m)` term in P11's equation.

### Optimization objectives and reliability definitions

**Direct.**

- Per-message reliability is `1 - epsilon`.
- Availability is `Pr(epsilon_t <= epsilon_threshold)`.
- A consecutive-outage counter `C_epsilon` tracks runs of messages whose error probability is
  above the threshold.
- Objective `F1` is the probability that the outage run exceeds `L_threshold`.
- Objective `F2` is energy `p_t m_t`.
- The paper writes a weighted mixed-integer problem that minimizes outage-burst risk and average
  energy subject to minimum availability and maximum power/blocklength.

### MDP and learning

**Direct.**

- **State:** instantaneous received SINR only.
- **Action:** continuous `(transmit power, blocklength)`.
- **Reward:** a negative indicator when the consecutive-outage run exceeds its threshold, plus a
  weighted, normalized energy-efficiency term `b / (p m)`.
- **Main algorithm:** Soft Actor-Critic with an actor, two Q critics, replay, and entropy
  regularization.
- **Constraint handling:** after evaluation, trained policies that satisfy the availability target
  in at least 90% of episodes are retained for trade-off analysis.

**Caveat.** The outage-run counter changes the reward but is omitted from the state. Unless it is
reconstructible from an unreported recurrent state, the written MDP is not Markov. Moreover,
post-hoc filtering of trained models is not constrained RL and supplies no guarantee that a deployed
policy will satisfy availability.

### Experimental settings

**Direct.**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Maximum received SNR | 20 dB |
| Maximum blocklength | 1,000 channel uses |
| Mean interferer INR | sampled uniformly from -10 to 5 dB |
| Payload | 50 bits |
| Interferers | 5 |
| Activation factor | 1 |
| Interferer message length | 10 minislots |
| Error threshold | `1e-5` |
| Availability threshold | 0.98 |
| Consecutive-outage threshold | 2 |
| Objective weights | outage 0.3; efficiency 0.7 |
| Evaluation | 1,000 episodes × 500 steps |
| Independent training trials | 20 per agent; nondominated solutions selected |
| Pareto experiment | 100 trials with randomized objective weights |

Neural/training details reported:

- SAC: separate actor and critics, two 256-unit ReLU layers, Adam `3e-4`, replay capacity `1e6`,
  entropy coefficient 0.2.
- DDPG: two hidden layers `(400, 300)`, Adam `1e-3`, replay `1e6`.
- TD3: the same `(400, 300)` scale is listed.
- A2C and PPO: shared two-layer, 64-unit networks; learning rates `7e-4` and `3e-4`,
  respectively; no replay.

Batch size, discount, target-update constants, PPO clipping/GAE settings, normalization details,
and several algorithm-specific settings are not reported.

### Results

**Direct.**

- Maximum-resource allocation satisfies availability in almost 99% of evaluated cases.
- At the 90th percentile, DDPG, TD3, and SAC meet the allowed unavailability of 0.02. Tabular
  Q-learning is just outside it; A2C, PPO, and random allocation fail it.
- Mean resource use normalized to maximum-resource allocation is reported as approximately:
  SAC 18%, DDPG 35%, Q-learning 45%, TD3 57%, PPO 64%, and A2C 75%.
- The observed maximum consecutive outage length is 9 for maximum resources; 10 for DDPG,
  SAC, TD3, and A2C; 12 for Q-learning; and 14 for PPO. Random allocation is worst.
- The authors state that fewer than ten consecutive outages occur in 99.9999% of evaluated cases
  for the strongest agents.
- SAC supplies the best reported Pareto trade-off.
- Mean training computation per step is reported as 11.538 ms for SAC, 6.467 ms for DDPG,
  6.072 ms for TD3, 2.205 ms for A2C, and 1.914 ms for PPO.

**Caveat.** These computation measurements are training costs, not policy-inference latency. The
evaluation horizon is too short to empirically validate a true `1e-5` or six-nines tail with tight
confidence, especially after selecting favorable runs.

### Assumptions and limitations

- Synthetic Rayleigh fading and uniformly sampled average interference are much simpler than an
  urban V2V channel and optical geometry.
- There is one desired link, no mobility, queue, retransmission, RF/VLC diversity, occlusion,
  packet deadline, or scheduler coupling.
- The availability threshold is 0.98 even though the per-message error threshold is `1e-5`; these
  are different reliability concepts and should not be conflated.
- Selecting nondominated models from repeated training trials may create winner-selection bias;
  uncertainty across all trials is not shown clearly.
- Reward semantics and written threshold semantics are awkward: penalizing only
  `C_epsilon > 2` does not “remove all consecutive outages.”
- It is an unreviewed preprint.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. Reuse the finite-blocklength normal approximation only where it matches the intended NR
   abstraction. Keep its output (conditional decoding error) separate from realized deadline miss,
   which also includes access, queue, processing, and transmission time.
2. Track miss bursts explicitly:
   `run_next = 0 if delivered_by_deadline else run + 1`.
   If run length affects reward or cost, it must be included in the observation (or reconstructed by a
   recurrent policy).
3. Report burst metrics—run-length CDF, maximum run, and probability of a run above `L`—in
   addition to marginal miss probability.
4. Replace post-hoc policy filtering with a genuine CMDP: a resource/quality reward, a distinct
   deadline-miss cost critic, and projected dual updates.
5. Do not read P11's PPO result as general evidence against PPO. The result is tied to an
   under-specified continuous-action benchmark and different network sizes/hyperparameters.
6. With three discrete choices `{RF, VLC, DUP}`, charge energy/resource cost for each attempted
   leg; DUP cost is the sum, even if the first copy arrives early.

## P12 — Generalization in RL for radio access networks

### Exact citation and access

Burak Demirel, Yu Wang, Cristian Tatino, and Pablo Soldati, “Generalization in Reinforcement
Learning for Radio Access Networks,” arXiv:2507.06602v3 [cs.LG], first submitted 9 July 2025,
revised 28 January 2026.

- arXiv record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06602>
- PDF: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06602>
- arXiv/DataCite DOI: <https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.06602>
- **Access depth:** complete v3 preprint, including supplementary architecture and
  hyperparameter tables, was read.
- **Publication status:** no peer-reviewed venue was identified.

### Problem and contribution

**Direct.** Most RAN RL policies are trained for one topology or operating regime and fail under
changed cell layouts, mobility, traffic, radio configurations, or UE types. The paper develops a
large-scale recipe for one generalized link-adaptation agent:

1. reconstruct state from static, semi-static, and dynamic radio features;
2. preserve feature age/staleness and recent HARQ/MCS history;
3. randomize physical, traffic, receiver, mobility, and cell configuration;
4. train with many distributed actors and a central prioritized-replay learner;
5. compare fixed-size MLP and graph encodings for topology generalization.

The case study is downlink MCS selection with HARQ. It is not a URLCC packet-routing or
constrained-RL study.

### Packet episode, action, and reward

**Direct.**

- One packet is one episode, continuing through ACK or drop after the permitted HARQ attempts.
- The text describes the 5G maximum as four retransmissions, or at most five decision steps.
- **Action:** one of 28 MCS indices from a 3GPP TS 38.214 256-QAM table. Transport-block size is
  fixed after the first attempt.
- **Reward:** `spectral_efficiency_n * ACK_n - alpha * n * NACK_n`.
  Spectral efficiency accounts for cumulative time-frequency resources through attempt `n`.
  Robustness coefficient `alpha` trades aggressive MCS/throughput against BLER/retransmission.

This is soft robustness shaping, not a reliability constraint.

### State construction

**Direct.**

Semi-static context includes serving and strongest interfering cells above a threshold; site
technology, carrier, antenna, geometry/inter-site distance, bandwidth, and transmit power; and UE
receiver/antenna type.

Dynamic state includes:

- current HARQ attempt;
- RSRP;
- the latest `K_CSI` CQI/rank samples;
- normalized buffer occupancy `min(N_TTI, buffer_bytes / TBS_max)`;
- rank-weighted average spectral efficiency;
- `K_H` ACK/NACK history samples;
- MCS and rank history.

Missing/aged categorical or hard-window values are replaced by `-1`; HARQ values are encoded as
ACK `+1`, NACK `-1`, then decay linearly toward zero with age. The paper's key systems insight is
that a value without its age can be actively misleading.

### Model and distributed training system

**Direct.**

- Main agent: DQN. The main MLP has six hidden layers of 256 units, with layer normalization in
  the best reported configuration, and 28 Q outputs.
- Graph alternatives separately encode static cell/neighbor context and combine it with dynamic
  features. One fully connected graph layer is evaluated using GCN or attention (GAT).
- Infrastructure: one central learner on an NVIDIA A100, 40 actors, 14 simulator environments per
  actor, and four prioritized replay shards with capacity four million each.
- Each actor generates about 132 transitions/s and pushes batches of 500.
- Replay warm-up is 45,000. Learner prefetch is 16 batches of 512, and it executes about 100
  batches/s (about 51,200 sampled transitions/s).
- Policy parameters are broadcast every 512 gradient steps, about every five seconds.
- Actor-specific fixed exploration follows an Ape-X-like exponentially spread epsilon schedule
  with base 0.4 and exponent coefficient 8.5.

Reported learner hyperparameters:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Discount | 1.0 |
| n-step | 1 |
| Actor local buffer | 500 |
| Hard target update | every 2,500 updates |
| Loss | MSE/Huber evaluated |
| Adam learning rate | `5e-5` |
| Adam betas / epsilon | 0.9, 0.999, `1e-4` |
| Weight decay | `0.02 / 512` |
| Max gradient norm | 20 |
| Learner batch | 512 |
| Priority exponent | 0.6 |
| Importance-sampling exponent | 0.4 |

### Training domain

**Direct.** A proprietary event-driven, 5G-compliant downlink simulator uses TDD at 3.5 GHz,
numerology 0, and SU-MIMO. Training uses three tri-sector sites with randomized MIMO/massive-MIMO
settings.

Randomized values include:

- cell radii `{166, 300, 600, 900, 1200}` m;
- bandwidth `{20, 40, 50, 80, 100}` MHz and corresponding subband counts
  `{51, 106, 133, 217, 273}`;
- downlink power `{20, 40, 50, 80, 100}` W;
- UE antennas `{2, 4}` and ranks `{2, 4}`;
- full-buffer/eMBB traffic and different UE counts;
- full-buffer mobility `{0.67, 10, 15, 30}` m/s and eMBB mobility `{0.67, 1.5, 3}` m/s;
- receiver types 0–3;
- indoor probabilities `{0.2, 0.4, 0.8}`;
- field-derived eMBB arrival behavior and 20–1,000 kB payloads.

Hyperparameter search uses 40,000 scenarios (160 seeds × 250 configurations), about 650 million
samples. The generalized model uses 240,000 scenarios (160 × 1,500), about three billion samples.

### Test regimes and baselines

**Direct.**

- B1: one cell, one full-buffer UE.
- B2: three-cell stable full-buffer MIMO/massive-MIMO.
- B3: three-cell dynamic eMBB.
- B4: unseen high mobility, 100–200 km/h.
- B5: nine-cell mixed full-buffer/eMBB plus unseen file-transfer/chat traffic.

The standard baseline is OLLA tuned to about 10% BLER. Specialized RL agents trained only in
the relevant full-buffer regime provide a second comparison.

### Results

**Direct.**

- The generalized model gains roughly 10% or more over OLLA in B2 and about 20% in B4 while
  matching specialized full-buffer agents.
- In dynamic regimes, the generalized policy's *gain over OLLA* is described as roughly four
  times the specialized agent's gain in B3 and twice its gain in B5. This is not a claim of fourfold
  absolute throughput.
- In B1, across UE distances 40–1,250 m and 50 simulations per position, generalized RL improves
  throughput by about 2–5.5%.
- Increasing robustness coefficient `alpha` from 0 through 0.5 to 2 lowers MCS/BLER and spectral
  efficiency; approximately 0.5 is the best throughput trade-off. In B4, the spectral-efficiency
  gain is about 40% at `alpha=0` and 15% at `alpha=2`.
- On the small three-cell problem, graph models add little. In the nine-cell setting, GAT is
  reported to deliver about 30% more throughput than MLP and GCN.

For the selected six-by-256 layer-normalized MLP (about 340,000 parameters; reported training time
34 h 14 min), gains over OLLA are:

| Benchmark | Throughput gain | Spectral-efficiency gain |
|---|---:|---:|
| B2 MIMO | 8.50% | 8.07% |
| B2 massive MIMO | 12.98% | 13.83% |
| B3 | 8.39% | 7.46% |
| B4 | 19.32% | 29.59% |
| B5 | 6.44% | 5.70% |

The reported platform includes an A100 40 GB, 48 CPU cores for the learner host, four cores per
replay shard, roughly 560 CPU cores for simulation, ZeroMQ, and an LSF cluster.

### Assumptions and limitations

- The simulator, field traffic model, data, and trained policy are proprietary; the paper does not
  provide an end-to-end reproducible artifact.
- The compute scale is orders of magnitude beyond the planned single-pair simulator.
- Training and test combinations differ, but many individual parameter values overlap; this is
  interpolation/combinatorial generalization plus a few extrapolations, not arbitrary zero-shot
  deployment.
- Only downlink MCS/HARQ is studied. There is no 1 ms end-to-end deadline, `1e-5` miss
  constraint, packet-duplication choice, sidelink, or VLC.
- The graph conclusion is demonstrated only up to nine cells.
- `gamma=1` is appropriate for a short, terminating per-packet episode; it should not be copied
  mechanically into a continuing average-cost CMDP.
- It remains a preprint.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. The most valuable idea is metadata-aware observation construction. Every measured RF/VLC
   feature should carry age or use an explicit missing value; retain short ACK/action histories and
   the current miss-run counter.
2. Domain-randomize within physical bounds: route/turn choices, inter-vehicle gap, blockage,
   traffic arrival phase, RF background load, weather/ambient light, optical alignment, and
   receiver noise. Hold out entire map seeds and combinations for testing.
3. Pre-generated stochastic traces can play the role of P12's distributed actors at vastly lower
   cost. Split traces by scenario seed before training so correlated windows do not leak into test.
4. An MLP is the default for a single V2V pair. Introduce variable-neighbor attention only after
   the environment includes interacting pairs and topology size truly varies.
5. Report in-distribution, held-combination, and genuine out-of-range tests separately. A single
   aggregate “generalization” number is not informative.

## P13 — Generalizing DRL for jammer-resilient frequency and power allocation

### Exact citation and access

Swatantra Kafle, Jithin Jagannath, Zackary Kane, Noor Biswas, Prem Sagar Pattanshetty Vasanth
Kumar, and Anu Jagannath, “Generalization of Deep Reinforcement Learning for Jammer-Resilient
Frequency and Power Allocation,” *IEEE Communications Letters*, vol. 27, no. 7, pp. 1789–1793,
July 2023.

- IEEE DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2023.3274594>
- arXiv: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02250>
- Author-hosted full text:
  <https://www.androcs.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/SKafleComLet2023.pdf>
- **Access depth:** complete five-page author manuscript was read and metadata was checked against
  the IEEE record.

### Problem and contribution

**Direct.** The paper asks whether separately trained distributed radio agents can be combined to
work in an unseen network topology. It trains DQN policies for jammer-resilient frequency and
power selection on several networks, averages parameters first at node level and then across
networks, and sequentially retrains the result to convergence.

This is parameter-sharing/model-averaging generalization, not adversarial training in a formal
minimax game.

### Network, observation, action, and reward

**Direct.**

- Multiple transmitter–receiver pairs communicate through a custom DS-CDMA model in
  MR-iNet Gym/ns3-gym under partial observation.
- Nodes move by random walk. Jammers can be constant, randomly frequency hopping, or chirped.
- Each distributed agent receives:
  - distances to the receivers of a fixed number of nearest neighbors;
  - local buffered-packet information;
  - interference it causes to neighbors;
  - locally sensed spectrum occupancy;
  - SINR feedback returned in receiver acknowledgements, with missing feedback encoded as `-1`.
- **Action:** a discrete combination of three power levels and two frequency choices, six actions.
- **Reward:** success receives `-C1 - C2`; failure receives `-C3`.
  `C1` is 0.05, 5, or 10 for low, medium, or high transmit power;
  `C2` is 0.05 or 2 for the frequency-selection penalty; and `C3=20`.

The frequency penalty is intended to discourage every agent choosing the same band. It is a
hand-shaped reward and supplies no reliability constraint.

### DQN and aggregation

**Direct.**

- Each DQN has hidden layers of 128 and 256 ReLU units and a linear action-value head.
- Adam learning rate is `1e-4`; action selection is epsilon-greedy and experience replay is used.
- Models are trained in five different source networks. Parameters are averaged at node level and
  then at network level, after which the aggregate is retrained until convergence.
- An unseen ten-node network is used for the main generalization test.
- TensorRT is used to deploy the learned model on embedded SDR hardware.

Discount, epsilon schedule, replay capacity, batch size, target-update rate, exact training counts,
and most radio values are missing from the short letter.

### Baselines and results

**Direct.** Baselines are greedy and random frequency/power allocation.

For three unseen hardware network configurations:

| Network | Proposed PDR | Greedy PDR | Random PDR | Proposed SE | Greedy SE | Random SE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 89.88% | 75.71% | 45.33% | 3.17 | 3.01 | 0.48 |
| 2 | 91.23% | 72.45% | 48.88% | 3.15 | 3.02 | 0.51 |
| 3 | 90.63% | 76.13% | 47.69% | 3.15 | 3.01 | 0.48 |

For jammer modes constant, random hopping, and chirp:

| Jammer | Proposed PDR | Greedy PDR | Random PDR | Proposed SE | Greedy SE | Random SE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Constant | 97.81% | 88.22% | 54.71% | 3.47 | 3.15 | 0.85 |
| Random hopping | 97.79% | 72.45% | 48.88% | 3.46 | 3.14 | 0.84 |
| Chirp | 97.89% | 76.13% | 47.69% | 3.47 | 3.14 | 0.84 |

The chirp setting uses period 14 and a 4.8 MHz/s rate. An outdoor demonstration with three
communication pairs and two jammers is described qualitatively.

### Assumptions and limitations

- Generalization is primarily to network size/topology under the same six-action radio abstraction.
- Nearest-`K` neighbor padding fixes input dimension but discards non-nearest aggregate
  interference; topology information is lossy.
- Parameter averaging assumes compatible representations and optimization basins. The authors
  explicitly leave convergence analysis open.
- The paper gives little information on source topologies, power values, seed counts, confidence
  intervals, hardware configuration, or policy inference latency.
- The PDR values are not URLLC-grade and have no end-to-end deadline.
- Jamming and DS-CDMA frequency allocation are distant from Manhattan NR sidelink + V-VLC.
- The outdoor demonstration is not a quantitative simulator-to-hardware validation with matched
  conditions.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. Do not incorporate federated averaging in the first single-pair implementation. Retain the idea
   as a later multi-fleet/domain generalization experiment.
2. A six-action DQN is a useful reminder that action combinations scale quickly; the planned
   three-action `{RF, VLC, DUP}` interface should remain deliberately small.
3. Test trained policies on entirely unseen geometry and load seeds. P13's main value is the
   experimental question—does the policy survive a changed network?—more than its averaging
   method.
4. If multi-pair interference is added, compare fixed nearest-neighbor features with permutation-
   invariant summaries or attention. Do not assume nearest `K` is sufficient.
5. TensorRT establishes feasibility of embedded inference in principle, but the paper reports no
   latency. Measure the actual exported model's p50/p99 inference time rather than citing P13.

## P14 — Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms

### Exact citation and access

John Schulman, Filip Wolski, Prafulla Dhariwal, Alec Radford, and Oleg Klimov, “Proximal Policy
Optimization Algorithms,” arXiv:1707.06347v2 [cs.LG], submitted 20 July 2017, revised
28 August 2017.

- arXiv record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347>
- PDF: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347>
- arXiv/DataCite DOI: <https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1707.06347>
- **Access depth:** complete technical report was read.
- **Publication status:** arXiv technical report, not a journal/conference publication.

### What PPO actually contributes

**Direct.** PPO is an on-policy policy-gradient method designed to take several minibatch gradient
steps on each newly collected batch without permitting the new policy to gain unlimited incentive
from a large probability-ratio change.

For old-policy probability `pi_old`, new probability `pi`, action `a_t`, state `s_t`, and advantage
estimate `A_t`, define:

```text
r_t(theta) = pi_theta(a_t | s_t) / pi_old(a_t | s_t)
L_clip(theta) =
    E[min(r_t A_t, clip(r_t, 1-epsilon, 1+epsilon) A_t)]
```

The minimum creates a pessimistic surrogate: positive-advantage actions stop receiving additional
surrogate improvement above `1+epsilon`, and negative-advantage actions stop benefiting when the
ratio falls below `1-epsilon`.

**Caveat.** Clipping is not a hard trust region and does not prevent all policy probabilities or KL
divergence from changing substantially. PPO's `epsilon` is also unrelated to a communications
error probability or CMDP constraint threshold.

### Value loss, entropy, and GAE

**Direct.** The combined actor–critic objective adds a squared value error and entropy bonus to the
clipped surrogate, with coefficients `c1` and `c2`. For a truncated rollout, GAE uses

```text
delta_t = reward_t + gamma * V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)
A_t = sum_l (gamma * lambda)^l * delta_{t+l}.
```

The paper's algorithm:

1. runs `N` parallel actors for `T` steps;
2. computes returns/advantages;
3. optimizes the surrogate for `K` epochs using minibatches of size `M`;
4. replaces the old policy and collects fresh trajectories.

An adaptive KL-penalty alternative adjusts a coefficient upward or downward when observed KL is
too large or small, but the clipped objective performs better in the reported study.

### Reproducible paper settings

**Direct.**

For MuJoCo:

- separate two-layer, 64-unit tanh policy and value networks;
- 2,048 rollout steps per update;
- Adam `3e-4`;
- 10 epochs and minibatches of 64;
- `gamma=0.99`, `lambda=0.95`, `epsilon=0.2`;
- no entropy bonus;
- one million timesteps, seven tasks, three seeds.

In the clipping sweep, normalized mean scores are 0.76, 0.82, and 0.70 for
`epsilon={0.1, 0.2, 0.3}`; removing clipping gives -0.39. Adaptive KL variants give roughly
0.68–0.74 and fixed KL penalties about 0.62–0.72.

For Atari:

- 49 games, 40 million frames, three trials;
- 8 actors × 128 steps;
- Adam `2.5e-4` annealed linearly;
- 3 epochs, batch 256;
- clipping 0.1 annealed linearly;
- value coefficient 1 and entropy coefficient 0.01.

Using average training reward, PPO wins 30 games versus 18 for ACER and one for synchronous A2C.
Using final evaluation over 100 episodes, PPO wins 19, ACER 28, A2C one, with one tie. PPO is
therefore not uniformly dominant under every evaluation convention.

### Assumptions and limitations

- PPO is empirically motivated; the clipped form does not inherit TRPO's monotonic-improvement
  guarantee.
- The paper has no task constraints, dual variable, separate cost advantage, or safe-RL proof.
- Three seeds are weak by current reproducibility standards.
- MuJoCo and Atari do not validate radio, deadline, rare-event, or nonstationary performance.
- Many current library defaults—value clipping, advantage normalization, orthogonal
  initialization, learning-rate schedules—are implementation conventions, not all claims of P14.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. A categorical three-action policy is a direct fit. Log the sampled action and its exact old log
   probability; PPO ratios are invalid if the behavior policy is reconstructed approximately.
2. Use vectorized, fresh trace segments and several epochs per batch. Do not add an off-policy
   replay buffer to PPO.
3. Compute GAE with separate masks for true termination and time-limit truncation so the value
   bootstrap is correct.
4. Add a separate cost value/advantage estimator. One practical constrained surrogate is based on
   `A_reward - lambda * A_cost`, with projected dual update
   `lambda <- max(0, lambda + eta * (J_cost - budget))`. This is an extension; P14 does not
   provide it.
5. Start with paper-like `clip=0.2`, `gamma=0.99`, and `lambda_GAE=0.95`, then tune against the
   actual decision interval and episode horizon. Track approximate KL, clipping fraction, policy
   entropy, explained variance, reward return, cost return, and dual value.
6. Keep the symbols distinct in code: e.g., `clip_eps` for PPO and `bler`/`miss_budget` for
   communications reliability.

## P15 — Dota 2 with Large Scale Deep Reinforcement Learning

### Exact citation and access

OpenAI: Christopher Berner, Greg Brockman, Brooke Chan, Vicki Cheung, Przemysław Dębiak,
Christy Dennison, David Farhi, Quirin Fischer, Shariq Hashme, Chris Hesse, Rafal Józefowicz,
Scott Gray, Catherine Olsson, Jakub Pachocki, Michael Petrov, Henrique P. d. O. Pinto, Jonathan
Raiman, Tim Salimans, Jeremy Schlatter, Jonas Schneider, Szymon Sidor, Ilya Sutskever, Jie Tang,
Filip Wolski, and Susan Zhang, “Dota 2 with Large Scale Deep Reinforcement Learning,”
arXiv:1912.06680v1 [cs.LG], 13 December 2019. The PDF cover is dated 10 March 2021.

- arXiv record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680>
- PDF: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.06680>
- arXiv/DataCite DOI: <https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.06680>
- **Access depth:** all main sections and technical appendices were read.
- **Publication status:** arXiv technical report.

### Why it is in this review

This paper is not about communications. Its relevance is PPO systems engineering: partial
observability, recurrent policies, asynchronous on-policy data quality, massive vectorization,
versioned environments, and continuing a training run through changing observation/action
schemas.

### Task and policy

**Direct.**

- Dota 2 is five versus five, partially observed, and typically lasts about 45 minutes or
  20,000 agent steps.
- The game runs at 30 frames/s; the policy acts every four frames, about every 0.133 s.
- The semantic observation contains roughly 16,000 values. Depending on action target choices,
  combinatorial action count is about 8,000–80,000.
- The environment supports 17 of 117 heroes and excludes several multi-unit and item mechanics.
  Purchases, ability leveling, courier control, and reserve-item management are scripted.
- Five hero controllers share one policy's parameters but maintain separate recurrent hidden
  states. A hero-identity feature lets the replicas choose different actions.
- The final recurrent policy is about 159 million parameters, dominated by one 4,096-unit LSTM;
  the LSTM is 84% of the parameter count.
- Inputs are semantic game-engine features, not pixels. They are broadly human-observable, but the
  agent sees all of them simultaneously whereas a human must move attention/click around.
- Policy and value heads share the LSTM. Target selection uses attention over unit embeddings and
  a learned validity mask; other action components are projections from recurrent state.

### PPO/self-play system

**Direct.**

- PPO with GAE trains from self-play. Current-policy self-play supplies 80% of games; the
  remaining 20% use historical opponents.
- Rollout CPUs run the game at about half real time and send observations to separate inference
  GPUs, which batch about 60 forward passes.
- Optimizer GPUs keep local experience buffers, sample 120 sequences of 16 steps, average
  gradients synchronously with all-reduce, and perform truncated backpropagation through time.
- At peak, 1,536 optimizer GPUs produce an effective batch of 2,949,120 timesteps by the main
  text's accounting. Table 2 shows the changing total range up to 3,145,728; the discrepancy
  reflects changing per-GPU samples/worker counts and the five-replica accounting note.
- Adam gradients are clipped per parameter to `±5 sqrt(v)`, where `v` is the running
  second-moment estimate before clipping.
- A new policy version is published every 32 gradient steps. Rollouts transmit 256-step segments
  and refresh parameters frequently.

### Reward and credit assignment

**Direct.** The objective is winning, but dense shaping handles very long credit assignment. The
reward table includes a team win reward of 5; solo death -1; team courier death -2; experience
0.002 per unit; gold gained 0.006; gold spent 0.0006; health and mana changes; Aegis 5; and
building objectives. Some counterintuitive negative weights offset double-counted gold/experience,
for example killed hero -0.6 and last hit -0.16.

Three transformations matter:

1. **Zero-sum:** subtract the opposing team's mean reward.
2. **Game-time weighting:** multiply every non-win reward at game time `T` by
   `0.6^(T / 10 minutes)` so late-game raw magnitudes do not dominate.
3. **Team spirit:** for individual raw reward `rho_i` and team mean `rho_bar`,
   `r_i = (1 - tau) rho_i + tau rho_bar`. `tau` rises during training so early individual
   credit is clearer and late behavior optimizes the team.

A sparse win/loss-only ablation with a one-hour horizon reaches TrueSkill about 155 and beats a
scripted agent, but is substantially less sample efficient than the shaped baseline.

### Reported hyperparameters

**Direct.**

| Parameter | Rerun | Evolving OpenAI Five |
|---|---:|---:|
| Frame skip | 4 | 4 |
| LSTM unroll / samples per segment | 16 / 16 | 16 / 16 |
| Optimizer GPUs | 512 | 480–1,536 |
| Samples per optimizer GPU | 120 | 120–128 |
| Total batch, timesteps | 983,040 | 983,040–3,145,728 |
| Rollout GPUs | 512 | 500–1,440 |

The paper defines real-time discount horizon as `H = step_time / (1 - gamma)`. A base agent with
180 s horizon (`gamma≈0.9993`) improves when resumed at horizons up to 6–12 minutes, with
diminishing returns.

### Data staleness and reuse

**Direct.**

- A sample generated by policy version `N` and optimized at version `M` has staleness `M-N`.
- Artificially adding about eight versions of staleness significantly slows training. The final
  design targets staleness 0–1 by sending about 30 s of game data and refreshing parameters about
  every minute.
- Sample reuse is optimizer consumption rate divided by new rollout production rate. Reusing data
  only two to three times causes about a factor-of-two slowdown in the reported early-training
  ablation; reuse near 6–8 can prevent a competent policy.
- The production system targets reuse near one.
- A Rerun batch of about 983k timesteps reaches an early TrueSkill threshold about 2.5 times
  faster than a 123k baseline, but speedup is sublinear and hyperparameters were not retuned for
  each batch.

These are unusually valuable negative results: for on-policy PPO, fresher data can matter more than
additional gradient computation on old samples.

### Surgery

**Direct.** More than twenty “surgeries” kept a single training run alive while game version,
observations, actions, reward details, and model architecture changed, approximately once every
one to two weeks.

Function-preserving examples include:

- append new observation inputs with zero outgoing input weights;
- add feed-forward units with random incoming weights and zero outgoing weights;
- expand the LSTM from 2,048 to 4,096 with very small random couplings so the recurrent state is
  nearly preserved;
- gradually anneal changed action/environment behavior from old to new;
- pause learning briefly after surgery;
- transform historical opponents so they remain compatible with the new environment.

Exact policy equivalence makes removing parameters difficult.

### Main outcomes and compute

**Direct.**

- On 13 April 2019, OpenAI Five beat reigning Dota 2 world champion OG 2–0.
- In the public Arena, it played 7,257 games against 3,193 teams and won 99.4%; 29 teams beat it
  in 42 games.
- Of its 7,215 counted wins, 3,140 were human abandonments, including some draft-stage exits.
  This materially qualifies the raw win rate.
- Estimated optimization compute at the OG match was `770 ± 50 PFLOP/s-days`; it reached
  `820 ± 50` when stopped on 22 April. These estimates exclude much rollout and experimental
  compute and have broad accounting uncertainty.
- A clean “Rerun” trained from scratch in the final environment used `150 ± 5 PFLOP/s-days`,
  around 20% of the evolving run's optimization compute, and eventually exceeded 98% win rate
  against final OpenAI Five.
- The main text gives Rerun dates 18 May–12 June 2019, while Appendix A gives
  18 May–12 July and the authors call the run two months. Appendix A and the two-month statement
  are mutually consistent; the June date appears to be a typo.

### Assumptions and limitations

- The task, scale, reward, and compute have no direct communications interpretation.
- Only one monumental evolving run and one clean rerun support many claims. Environment changes,
  surgery, batch, horizon, model size, and other hyperparameters are confounded.
- Many architecture decisions were historical and too expensive to ablate.
- The 17-hero, scripted-action environment is a restricted subset of Dota 2.
- Semantic features supply a different attention burden from human play.
- The Arena rate includes a very large number of abandonments.
- The hypothetical “40 months if restarted after every surgery” is a naive multiplication, not a
  controlled experiment.
- Surgery is most useful while a costly environment is evolving. The authors explicitly see little
  need for it when a final, understood environment is available from the start.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. Borrow operational discipline, not scale: version trace schema, environment physics, scenario
   configuration, policy code, seeds, normalization statistics, and checkpoint metadata together.
2. With inexpensive simulation, restart after major semantic changes until the model is stable.
   Add schema-migration/surgery tools only when old training has become genuinely costly.
3. PPO rollouts should record behavior-policy version. Monitor staleness and do not repeatedly
   optimize the same trace batch merely because spare GPU time is available.
4. Recurrent PPO is justified only if a fixed history stack cannot expose channel/occlusion
   dynamics. Begin with a small MLP plus explicit lagged features; a 4,096-unit LSTM has no
   relevance to this simulator.
5. Preserve a clean, immutable evaluation environment and compare checkpoints on the same trace
   seeds. P15's evolving-environment evaluation illustrates why otherwise learning curves can be
   misleading.
6. PPO clip 0.2 and GAE lambda 0.95 are useful precedents, not evidence that the same horizon or
   batch scale is optimal for 1 ms packet decisions.

## P16 — Adaptive tuning of parameterized traffic controllers via MARL

### Exact citation and access

### Problem and contribution

**Direct.** Fast state-feedback traffic controllers react cheaply but rely on parameters tuned for
one regime. Direct RL control can adapt but has a larger action rate/dimension and weaker fallback
behavior. The proposed hierarchy puts:

- conventional PI traffic controllers on the lower, fast time scale; and
- independent DDPG agents on the upper, slow time scale to retune controller parameters.

One agent tunes each controller using local observations, while every agent receives the same
global congestion/smoothness reward. Training and deployment are decentralized by agent. The
paper argues that unaffected local controllers continue operating if one adaptive agent is disturbed.

### Traffic model and controllers

**Direct.**

- A multi-class METANET macroscopic freeway model has two vehicle classes.
- One origin splits into primary and secondary routes, each with an on-ramp; both routes rejoin at
  one destination.
- Weather changes model parameters. Good-to-bad weather lowers critical density and free-flow
  speed and increases speed-relaxation time.
- Simulation step is 10 s.

The route controller is PI-DTA:

```text
u_dta(k) = u_dta(k-1)
           + K_P (DeltaT(k) - DeltaT(k-1))
           + K_I DeltaT(k)
```

where `u_dta` is the fraction choosing the primary route and `DeltaT` is the estimated travel-time
difference.

Each ramp uses PI-ALINEA:

```text
u_rm(k+1) = u_rm(k)
            + K_R (rho_bar - rho_b(k))
            - K_A (rho_b(k) - rho_b(k-1)).
```

Both physical control inputs are clipped to `[0,1]`.

### Agents, observations, and actions

**Direct.**

- Agent 1 tunes PI-DTA parameters `[K_P, K_I]`.
- Agents 2 and 3 independently tune `[rho_bar, K_R, K_A]` for the two PI-ALINEA ramps.
- Agent 1 observes class-specific demand and queues at its origin, current and previous route
  travel-time differences, current route split, and weather indicator.
- Each ramp agent observes class-specific local demand/queue, current and previous downstream
  bottleneck density, current ramp input, and weather indicator.
- The centralized single-agent baseline concatenates every local observation and tunes all eight
  parameters jointly.
- Observations are normalized to similar numerical orders before actor/critic input.

Action bounds are:

```text
K_P       in [0, 0.5]
K_I       in [0, 0.1]
K_A       in [0, 0.1]
K_R       in [0, 0.05]
rho_bar   in [15, 50] vehicles/km/lane
```

### Reward

**Direct.** Every agent receives the same negative global reward accumulated over one slow RL
interval:

```text
- sum_k [
    omega_TTS * total_time_spent(k)
    + omega_u * ||u(k) - u(k-1)||_2^2
  ].
```

The weights are `omega_TTS=3.33e-4` and `omega_u=2.22e-5`. Thus the agents jointly minimize
total vehicle-hours and rapid changes in the lower-layer control signals.

### DDPG training and time-scale separation

**Direct.**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Training episodes | 5,000 |
| Minibatch | 64 |
| Replay capacity | `1e4` |
| Discount | 0.99 |
| Actor/critic learning rate | 0.001 |
| Target-network soft-update rate | 0.01 |
| Exploration-noise standard deviation | 0.3 |
| Exploration-noise decay | `5e-5` |

The paper does not state hidden-layer sizes or activation functions.

Time scales:

- METANET simulation: 10 s;
- PI-DTA route split: 300 s;
- PI-ALINEA ramp rates: 60 s;
- DDPG parameter tuning: 1,800 s.

This is a 3×/30× separation between lower controllers and their relevant RL update interval, and
180 simulator steps per RL action.

### Scenarios and baselines

**Direct.**

- Each episode/case study lasts 5.5 h.
- Weather switches from good to bad at minute 166.
- Origin demand profiles receive additive Gaussian noise with origin/class-specific standard
  deviations, followed by a third-order Butterworth low-pass filter with normalized cutoff 0.1.
- Fixed-controller parameters are
  `K_P=0.01`, `K_I=0.005`, both `K_A=0.1`, both `K_R=0.005`, and both desired densities
  `37.5 vehicles/km/lane`.
- No control uses route split 0.5 and leaves both ramps fully open.
- Ten independent multi-agent and ten independent single-agent frameworks are trained.
  For the main table, each method's representative trained framework is the one whose mean TTS is
  closest to the ten-framework mean. That selected framework is evaluated across 100 stochastic
  runs.

For robustness, multiplicative noise is applied after normalization to the PI-DTA observation,
starting at minute 30:

```text
eta_i = 1 + alpha_i/100
alpha_i ~ clipped Normal(0, sigma), clipped to [-100, 100]
sigma in {0, 25, 50, 75, 100}.
```

In the decentralized case this corrupts agent 1; in the centralized case it corrupts part of the one
agent's joint observation.

### Results

**Direct.**

| Controller | Total time spent (vehicle-hours) |
|---|---:|
| Multi-agent adaptive | `7544.1 ± 41.1` |
| Single-agent adaptive | `7583.1 ± 38.1` |
| Fixed parameters | `7656.7 ± 42.5` |
| No control | `8054.3 ± 36.8` |

**Calculated.** From those table means, multi-agent adaptive tuning improves TTS by about 1.47%
over fixed parameters and 6.34% over no control. Its mean is about 0.51% below the centralized
single-agent value. These are calculations, not percentages quoted by the paper.

### Assumptions and limitations

- Decentralized agents coordinate only through a global reward. Training is not a centralized
  critic/CTDE construction, despite the central unit needed to calculate and broadcast reward.
- The disturbance experiment corrupts only the route-controller observation, a setup structurally
  favorable to isolation. It does not simulate agent dropout, corrupt global reward, or disturbances
  affecting every local sensor.
- Selecting the trained seed nearest the average is defensible as a representative example but
  discards the full seed distribution in the main comparison.
- No statistical hypothesis test or confidence interval for method differences is reported.
- There is one macroscopic network and one designed demand/weather profile.
- Hidden architecture, initialization, action scaling details, and inference time are absent from the
  paper, although code is linked.
- DDPG is unconstrained and optimizes a soft congestion/smoothness reward. There is no safety,
  reliability, or hard traffic constraint.
- Parameter tuning every 30 minutes is a supervisory-control problem, not millisecond packet
  selection.

### Concrete transfer to the planned simulator

**Inference / transfer.**

1. The transferable pattern is a **slow supervisory layer over a trusted fast fallback**. A later
   extension could let RL slowly tune a heuristic's duplication threshold, link margin, or dual
   step size while a deterministic per-packet rule remains available.
2. Do not use P16 as the main architecture for 1 ms `{RF,VLC,DUP}` selection. Its action interval
   is 30 minutes and its DDPG actions are continuous controller gains.
3. Fault isolation becomes relevant only in a multi-pair/multi-intersection simulator. When it is
   added, corrupt one sensor, one agent, global reward, and common observations in separate tests
   so resilience is not built into the benchmark definition.
4. A smoothness/switching penalty is applicable: frequent RF↔VLC switching may cause signaling,
   reconfiguration, or beam/acquisition costs. Keep this performance penalty separate from the
   deadline-miss constraint.
5. Include fixed-parameter and no-adaptation controllers even when comparing against RL. P16's
   gains are modest, and such baselines reveal whether learning complexity is justified.

## Cross-paper synthesis for the Manhattan NR-sidelink + V-VLC constrained-RL simulator

Everything in this section is **inference / transfer**, not a direct claim shared by all eight papers.

### What each paper should contribute—and what it should not

| Paper | Reuse | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|
| P9 | Duplication externalities; first-arrival logic; network/load-aware selection | soft reward as a reliability guarantee; inconsistent traffic units |
| P10 | lagged-KPI prediction gate; useful/unnecessary DUP diagnostics | current-packet leakage; 2 ms/95.8% as URLLC proof |
| P11 | finite-blocklength abstraction; outage-run metrics | post-hoc constraint filtering; state without run counter |
| P12 | age-aware observations; domain randomization; held-out scenarios | billion-sample scale; graph model for a single pair |
| P13 | unseen-topology evaluation; compact discrete radio action precedent | naive parameter averaging in the first implementation |
| P14 | categorical PPO, GAE, clipped surrogate, on-policy batch loop | treating clip epsilon as a safety bound |
| P15 | versioning, recurrent-data hygiene, staleness/reuse monitoring | enormous LSTM/batch/compute; surgery before it is needed |
| P16 | slow adaptive tuning over a trusted fast controller; fault isolation | 30-minute DDPG hierarchy for per-packet control |

### Proposed packet-level environment contract

At each packet decision:

1. expose only pre-action observations;
2. choose one categorical action: `RF`, `VLC`, or `DUP`;
3. evaluate both link traces internally, but reveal only feedback that the selected action would
   generate;
4. compute first successful arrival and deadline status;
5. charge every attempted resource/energy cost;
6. update ACK histories, feature ages, queues/load, and consecutive-miss counter.

A compact observation should include:

- geometry/kinematics available to the controller: distance, relative speed/heading, intersection
  phase or map-relative position;
- RF: current/lagged SINR or RSRP proxy, channel busy ratio/interference/load, queue/age;
- VLC: current/lagged received optical power/SNR, incidence/irradiance angles or alignment proxy,
  line-of-sight/occlusion belief, ambient/weather indicator, queue/age;
- recent action and ACK/deadline outcome;
- consecutive-miss run;
- normalized time-to-deadline or packet class if those vary.

Hidden simulator state may contain exact obstruction and instantaneous fading, but it must not leak
into the observation unless a real sensor/estimator would provide it.

### Recommended reward/cost separation

Use a scalar performance reward for resource efficiency and operational preferences, for example:

```text
reward =
    - energy_or_airtime(action)
    - switch_weight * I[action != previous_action]
    - optional_latency_weight * clipped_latency
```

Define the safety cost independently:

```text
cost = I[packet not delivered by deadline].
```

For DUP, resource cost is the sum of RF and VLC attempts, even if one arrives first. If the
implementation permits early cancellation, model the cancellation timing and signaling rather than
silently assuming zero cost.

Train PPO with reward and cost value heads and a projected dual variable. A common sign
convention is to maximize an advantage such as
`A_reward - lambda * A_cost` and update
`lambda <- max(0, lambda + dual_lr * (empirical_cost - cost_budget))`.
The exact estimator, time scale, and whether the budget is discounted or average cost must be
specified in the work plan; none of P9–P16 supplies that complete design.

### Baseline suite justified by these papers

1. always RF;
2. always VLC;
3. always DUP;
4. oracle per-packet minimum-resource action using hidden counterfactual outcomes—upper bound,
   not deployable;
5. threshold heuristic based on observed RF/VLC margins and blockage;
6. P10-style lagged-KPI deadline predictor plus DUP gate;
7. unconstrained PPO with a weighted miss penalty;
8. Lagrangian constrained PPO;
9. optional recurrent constrained PPO if explicit history stacking proves inadequate.

Report both reliability and resource consequences: deadline-miss probability, Wilson or
Clopper–Pearson interval, p50/p95/p99/p99.9 latency where support permits, energy/airtime, RF/VLC
utilization, DUP rate, action switching, useful-DUP rate, false-positive/false-negative DUP gate
rates, and outage-run distribution.

### Rare-event evaluation warning

A target near `1e-5` cannot be credibly evaluated with a few thousand packets. Zero observed
misses does not mean zero miss probability. Under the rule-of-three approximation, observing zero
misses in `N` independent trials gives an approximate 95% upper bound of `3/N`; reaching an upper
bound below `1e-5` requires more than about 300,000 effectively independent packets, and
correlation/bursts reduce effective sample size. Use many held-out trace seeds, report confidence
intervals, and separately show burst/run behavior.

### Reproducibility checklist before coding begins

- Pin equation conventions and SI units in one configuration schema.
- Resolve RF and VLC common-cause correlation rather than multiplying marginal error rates.
- Version trace generator and trace schema; store scenario seed and physics/config hash.
- Split train/validation/test by scenario seed or full trajectory, never by adjacent packet rows.
- Record every action's old log probability and policy version.
- Save observation normalization with checkpoints.
- Distinguish `terminated` from time-limit `truncated`.
- Evaluate all baselines on identical immutable trace sets.
- Retain a unit test for DUP first-arrival, duplicate-discard, cost accounting, feature aging, and
  miss-run recurrence.
- Label every comparison as in-distribution, held-combination, or extrapolation.

## Unresolved source issues

1. **P9:** mathematical superscripts in the repository PDF are corrupted in text extraction.
   Reliability targets can be reconstructed from the reported shaping weights, but the rounded
   exponent labels should be checked visually against a clean IEEE PDF before exact quotation.
2. **P9:** packet/load units conflict among the table, body, and figures (Kbytes versus bytes and
   kB/s versus MB/s). No consistent interpretation makes all labels correct.
3. **P9:** the combined-error equation's event convention is not recoverable safely from the
   damaged font extraction; implement explicit OR-success semantics instead.
4. **P10:** actual random-forest structure and train/test protocol are not disclosed.
5. **P11–P12:** both are preprints without a peer-reviewed venue identified at the access date.
6. **P11:** written state omits the consecutive-outage counter used in reward; no recurrent policy
   is described.
7. **P13:** important radio/training/deployment settings and inference timing are absent from the
   short letter.
8. **P15:** Rerun end date conflicts between the main text (June) and compute appendix (July).
9. **P16:** proceedings DOI/pages were not yet available from the arXiv record; the paper only
   reports acceptance to ECC 2026.
