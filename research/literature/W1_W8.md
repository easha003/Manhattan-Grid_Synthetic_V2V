# Deep reading notes for workbook entries W1–W8

Date reviewed: 2026-07-28

## Scope and evidence standard

These notes resolve the shortened workbook titles to exact records, then use the fullest legally accessible manuscript for the technical reading. The spreadsheet's own summaries were used only to identify the entries; they are not treated as evidence.

Notation used below:

- **Paper claim** means the statement, number, interpretation, or conclusion is made by the paper.
- **Reviewer assessment** means a conclusion drawn in this review from the disclosed formulation, plots, or missing information. It is not attributed to the authors.
- **Access depth** distinguishes a complete manuscript from an abstract or metadata record. All eight entries were read at full-manuscript depth. W5 is itself only a two-page work-in-progress paper, and W6 is currently a preprint; those are properties of the works, not access failures.

The planned work used for the overlap/gap analysis is understood to be a packet-level vehicular controller in a Manhattan-grid urban scenario, with an NR sidelink RF leg and a vehicle-to-vehicle VLC leg; actions include RF, VLC, and duplicate transmission; blockage can be caused by a third vehicle; and reliability/latency are handled as explicit CMDP costs or constraints rather than only as a scalar reward. If that scope changes, the comparison section should be revised.

## W1 — Deep Reinforcement Learning Algorithms for Hybrid V2X Communication: A Benchmarking Study

### Exact record and access

**Published record:** Fouzi Boukhalfa, Réda Alami, Mastane Achab, Eric Moulines, Mehdi Bennis, and Thierry Lestable, “Deep Reinforcement Learning Algorithms for Hybrid V2X Communication: A Benchmarking Study,” *2024 IEEE International Conference on Communications Workshops (ICC Workshops)*, Denver, CO, USA, pp. 1956–1961. [DOI 10.1109/ICCWorkshops59551.2024.10615405](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCWorkshops59551.2024.10615405).

**Full-text sources read:**

- [University of Oulu repository record](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/handle/10024/53996) and its [open full text](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/bitstream/10024/53996/1/nbnfioulu-202502061487.pdf)
- [arXiv:2310.03767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03767)

**Version caveat:** the arXiv preprint lists the first five authors; the final conference record adds Thierry Lestable. Use the six-author final citation in the bibliography. **Access depth: complete paper**, cross-checked against the final repository copy.

### Research question and contribution

The paper asks which of four established deep-RL algorithms is most suitable for choosing among DSRC, front/rear vehicular VLC, and combinations of those links as two vehicles move along a highly curved road. It is primarily an **algorithm benchmark**, not a new channel model or a new RL algorithm.

Its contributions are:

1. A common discrete MDP for hybrid DSRC/V-VLC selection.
2. A side-by-side comparison of PPO, TRPO, Rainbow DQN, and a discrete-action implementation of SAC.
3. Evaluation of reliability, VLC use, redundant use, erroneous taillight use, switching count, convergence, policy stability, and transfer to a more difficult road.

The paper builds directly on the “multi-Q regressor” strategy of Schettler et al., which it uses as its state-of-the-art comparison.

### Scenario, mobility, traffic, and communication assumptions

**Paper specification**

- Two vehicles form a leader/follower pair in SUMO and traverse a “serpentine” road.
- Scenario 1, used for training and main testing, has relatively few hairpin curves. Scenario 2 has more hairpins and is used as an out-of-distribution robustness test.
- Vehicle speed is 30–40 km/h.
- Each run lasts 400 s.
- Packets are 1,024 bytes and are generated at 10 Hz.
- DSRC transmit power is 20 mW.
- Nominal DSRC and VLC rates are 6 Mbit/s and 1 Mbit/s, respectively.
- The VLC simulation uses Veins-VLC and empirical HELLA headlamp radiation patterns, including the asymmetry and directionality of real automotive lights. The transmitter may use headlight or taillight VLC.

The selected communication action does not change the vehicle trajectories. Consequently, the paper simplifies the transition law from \(P(s'|s,a)\) to \(P(s'|s)\).

**Reviewer assessment**

- This is a very small communication topology: one transmitter–receiver relation and no competing packet queues, explicit co-channel network, intersections, buildings, or third-vehicle occluders.
- Mobility is dynamic, but the state is essentially geometric and privileged. It is not a partially observed radio controller based on measurable link statistics.
- The empirical beam pattern is more valuable for validating VLC geometry than a generic symmetric Lambertian headlamp would be.

### MDP formulation

#### State

The four-dimensional state is the transmitter position normalized and clipped by the nominal DSRC range \(R=10^3\) m, plus an angle represented by its cosine and sine:

\[
s_t=(X_t,Y_t,\cos\phi_t,\sin\phi_t).
\]

The source's vector notation is difficult to parse in the extracted manuscript, but its prose and figure make the intent clear: encode position and orientation/relative angle while avoiding angular wraparound.

The authors explicitly acknowledge that this state omits:

- other vehicles and surrounding geometry;
- prior states, prior communication outcomes, and history;
- channel properties and communication quality;
- richer environmental context.

That admission is important: the most direct prior paper does **not** solve observable-state, interference-aware, history-aware RAT selection.

#### Actions

The eight actions are:

| Index | Action |
|---|---|
| \(a_1\) | no transmission |
| \(a_2\) | DSRC |
| \(a_3\) | VLC headlight |
| \(a_4\) | DSRC + headlight |
| \(a_5\) | VLC taillight |
| \(a_6\) | DSRC + taillight |
| \(a_7\) | taillight + headlight |
| \(a_8\) | all available technologies |

This is the strongest action-space precedent in W1–W8 for RF-only, VLC-only, and duplicate RF+VLC transmission. It is broader than the planned minimal three-action controller because it includes “no transmission” and two optical directions.

#### Reward

The paper defines

\[
r(s,a)=p(s,a)-C(a),
\]

where \(p(s,a)\) is communication-success probability and \(C(a)\) is communication cost.

**Reproducibility limitation:** it does not disclose a numerical cost table or a complete computable definition of \(p(s,a)\) in the paper. Therefore this equation communicates the preference ordering but is not enough to reproduce the experiment faithfully.

There is no separate reliability cost signal, Lagrange multiplier, feasible-set projection, or hard chance constraint. Reliability and resource use are scalarized in the reward.

### Algorithms and hyperparameters

The four evaluated algorithms and disclosed grid searches are:

| Algorithm | Hyperparameter grid |
|---|---|
| Rainbow DQN | learning rate \(\{0.1,0.3\}\); weight decay \(\{0.5,0.7\}\); atom count \(\{25,100\}\); prioritized-replay epsilon \(\{10^{-5},10^{-4}\}\) |
| TRPO | KL/trust-region upper bound \(\{0.005,0.01\}\); network depth/width value reported as \(\{32,64\}\); line-search maximum iterations \(\{10,20\}\) |
| SAC | soft-update coefficient \(\{0.005,0.01\}\); learning rate \(\{3\times10^{-4},5\times10^{-4}\}\); batch size \(\{64,128,256\}\) |
| PPO | actor learning rate \(\{10^{-5},10^{-2}\}\); critic learning rate \(\{10^{-3},10^{-2}\}\); clipping epsilon \(\{0.2,0.3\}\) |

Reported network structures are:

- PPO actor: 4–64–64–8; critic: 4–64–64–1; 9,545 total parameters.
- SAC actor and critic: 4–256–256–8; 276,512 reported parameters.
- Rainbow: value stream 4–128–128–25 and advantage stream 4–128–128–200; 62,689 parameters.
- TRPO actor: 4–64–8; critic: 4–64–1; 1,225 parameters.

SAC and Rainbow converge in fewer than 100 episodes in the shown learning curves; PPO and TRPO converge more slowly. The paper nevertheless favors PPO overall because its policy switches less and appears more stable.

### Evaluation and reported results

#### Main scenario

| Metric | PPO | Multi-Q baseline | SAC | Rainbow DQN | TRPO |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reliability/PDR | 98.49% | 99.5% | 98.52% | 97.57% | 95.51% |
| VLC utilization | 93.68% | 58% | 92.50% | 91.24% | 95.35% |
| Non-redundant decisions | 99.97% | 99.4% | 100% | 99.14% | 100% |
| Taillight use | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0.85% | 0% |
| Number of switches | 8 | not reported | 27 | 29 | 15 |

Learned action shares provide useful behavioral detail:

- PPO: approximately 7% DSRC, 91% headlight, 1.5% DSRC+headlight, and essentially no other action.
- SAC: 0.02% no-send, 8.29% DSRC, and 91.67% headlight.
- TRPO: 0.02% no-send, 5.14% DSRC, and 94.82% headlight.
- Rainbow: 8.27% DSRC, 90.77% headlight, 0.44% DSRC+taillight, 0.38% dual optical, and 0.11% all-links.

Thus, in this lightly loaded two-car setting, the learned solution is mostly “use headlight VLC, fall back to DSRC,” not frequent duplication.

#### Robustness scenario

The paper reports the reliability of the best and second-best tuned model for each algorithm on the more difficult Scenario 2:

| Model rank | PPO | SAC | Rainbow DQN | TRPO |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Best training model | 84.84% | 94.78% | 71.84% | 78.81% |
| Second-best training model | 97.57% | 97.71% | 95.62% | 95.27% |

**Reviewer assessment:** the reversal between “best” and “second-best” models is a strong sign of hyperparameter/model-selection overfitting and weak robustness. It also means the final statement that PPO is best in reliability, robustness, complexity, stability, and switching should be read as a multi-criterion author judgment, not as a result uniformly supported by every number: SAC has slightly higher main-scenario reliability, far better best-model transfer, and faster convergence, whereas PPO switches much less.

### URLLC treatment

The introduction cites extreme V2X reliability ambitions, but the experiment has:

- no finite-blocklength decoding model;
- no packet deadline or lateness event;
- no queue or arrival-process model beyond 10-Hz packet generation;
- no end-to-end delay distribution;
- no explicit reliability constraint;
- achieved PDR of roughly 95.5–98.5%, not five or more “nines.”

Therefore, **paper claim:** the method targets reliable hybrid V2X communication. **Reviewer assessment:** it is not a URLLC feasibility demonstration and should not be cited as satisfying \(10^{-4}\) or \(10^{-5}\) packet-failure constraints.

### Main limitations

1. One leader/follower pair and a specialized curvy-road geometry.
2. DSRC rather than NR sidelink.
3. No explicit interference or network-load state.
4. No third-vehicle, building, or stochastic blockage process.
5. A privileged four-variable geometry state with no history.
6. Reward probability and link-cost terms are insufficiently specified for exact reproduction.
7. Reliability is an objective metric, not a CMDP constraint.
8. Robustness results reveal sensitivity to model selection.
9. No statistical uncertainty or rare-event evaluation suitable for ultra-high reliability.

### Relationship to the planned implementation

**Direct overlap**

- Hybrid RF/VLC choice at each decision instant.
- An action that sends the same information over RF and VLC.
- PPO as a leading discrete-control candidate.
- Metrics for switch count, redundancy fraction, PDR, and link-use share.
- Empirical automotive headlamp beam patterns and their asymmetry.

**Decisive gap**

- NR sidelink replaces DSRC.
- Manhattan-grid turns, buildings, and a third-vehicle occluder replace a two-car serpentine road.
- Observable channel/load/history variables replace privileged position-only state.
- A constrained objective replaces an undocumented scalar probability-minus-cost reward.
- Deadline-aware packet success replaces aggregate PDR alone.

### Coding implications

1. Preserve a minimal action map such as `{RF, VLC, DUP}` and optionally compare against W1's broader eight-action map in an ablation.
2. Implement switch count and duplicate-use fraction as first-class evaluation metrics.
3. Include W1-like PPO as a baseline, but compare it with the selected constrained method under identical seeds and state information.
4. Validate the VLC model against an empirical/asymmetric beam pattern; do not assume a symmetric Lambertian source is automatically representative of vehicle headlights.
5. Make link-success probabilities and action costs explicit, unit-tested functions.
6. Test across held-out maps, turn patterns, densities, and blocker processes so that W1's robustness reversal is not repeated.

## W2 — DRL-Based RAT Selection in a Hybrid Vehicular Communication Network

### Exact record and access

Badreddine Yacine Yacheur, Toufik Ahmed, and Mohamed Mosbah, “DRL-Based RAT Selection in a Hybrid Vehicular Communication Network,” *2023 IEEE 97th Vehicular Technology Conference (VTC2023-Spring)*, Florence, Italy, pp. 1–5. [DOI 10.1109/VTC2023-Spring57618.2023.10199400](https://doi.org/10.1109/VTC2023-Spring57618.2023.10199400).

**Full text read:** [arXiv:2407.00828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00828). The conference paper appeared in 2023; the openly posted arXiv author copy is dated 2024. **Access depth: complete conference manuscript.**

### Research question and contribution

This work learns a per-vehicle radio-access strategy for a hybrid network containing two RF technologies:

- ITS-G5/IEEE 802.11p;
- LTE-V2X PC5 in centrally scheduled mode 3.

It is important for the planned work because the agent can select either single RAT, duplicate a packet over both RATs, or divide traffic across them. It is **not** an RF/VLC paper, so it does not capture complementarity from fundamentally different propagation modes.

The stated goal is to maintain application reliability while reducing unnecessary duplicate transmissions. The authors compare their DDQN strategy against fixed single-RAT operation, static two-RAT duplication, and a TOPSIS multi-criteria selection method.

### Scenario, mobility, traffic, and radio assumptions

**Simulation stack and road**

- OMNeT++ with SUMO, Artery, and SimuLTE.
- Straight 2-km, two-way highway.
- Two lanes in each direction and two base stations.
- No buildings or other structural blockage.
- A five-vehicle platoon with 10-m inter-vehicle spacing under CACC.
- Platoon vehicles travel at 10 m/s; other vehicles travel at 20 m/s.
- Each vehicle broadcasts every 100 ms.

**ITS-G5 parameters**

- EDCA MAC.
- 5,875–5,885 MHz.
- 23 dBm transmit power.
- \(-85\) dBm receiver sensitivity and energy-detection threshold.
- \(-90\) dBm noise power.
- Constant-speed propagation model as stated by the paper.

**LTE-V2X parameters**

- Sidelink semi-persistent scheduling, PC5 mode 3.
- 5,895–5,905 MHz.
- 23 dBm transmit power.
- \(-85\) dBm receiver sensitivity and energy-detection threshold.
- \(-110\) dBm noise power.
- Jakes fading model.

The agent uses ACK/reception reports carried via ITS-G5. The paper assumes end-to-end delay remains below the 100-ms inter-packet period so that state/reward timing is well ordered.

**Reviewer assessment**

- The two links are both near-5.9-GHz RF legs and may experience correlated congestion/propagation failures. They are not a substitute for modeling RF/VLC diversity.
- Mode-3 LTE sidelink assumes infrastructure scheduling, unlike autonomous NR sidelink mode 2.
- Per-packet ACK/reception reports from all surrounding vehicles supply extensive feedback and add unmodeled overhead.

### Decision process

#### State and timing

The vehicle's state is

\[
s_t=[\mathrm{SNIR}_{\mathrm{ITS-G5}},
\mathrm{SNIR}_{\mathrm{LTE}},
\mathrm{PRR}_{\mathrm{ITS-G5}},
\mathrm{PRR}_{\mathrm{LTE}},
L,R],
\]

where \(L\) and \(R\) encode the application's latency and reliability requirements.

The paper distinguishes three moments:

1. \(s_t\): observation immediately before making the transmission decision;
2. \(s'_t\): observation when the earliest reception information arrives, used in reward construction;
3. \(s_{t+1}\): the latest observation before the next packet is sent.

That delayed-observation distinction is one of the work's most useful ideas for a real packet controller.

#### Actions

| Index | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | single ITS-G5 |
| 1 | single LTE-V2X |
| 2 | hybrid redundant transmission |
| 3 | hybrid traffic division |

“Redundant” means the same information is sent on both legs; “division” distributes data between legs.

#### Reward

The reward combines:

- reception state over surrounding vehicles;
- a performance-satisfaction term \(PS\), intended to reflect application reliability/latency requirements;
- a link-quality improvement term \(LQ\), comparing SNIR between \(s_t\) and \(s'_t\).

The displayed form is

\[
R_{V_i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\ne i}\mathrm{ReceptionState}[V_i][V_j]
+\frac{\alpha}{2}PS+\frac{\beta}{2}LQ.
\]

For a receiver, `ReceptionState` is 2 if a duplicate is received, 1 if the packet is received exactly once, and 0 if it is missed. A separate success-rate counter increments only when all surrounding vehicles receive the message exactly once.

**Reproducibility limitations**

- The complete equations for \(PS\) and \(LQ\) are not provided.
- Numerical \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are not disclosed.
- Rewarding a duplicate reception with 2 while separately favoring “exactly once” makes the resource/reliability trade-off difficult to infer from the formula alone.

### DDQN implementation

The paper describes:

- behavior and target networks;
- experience replay;
- epsilon-greedy action selection;
- mean-squared Bellman loss;
- two hidden layers of 256 ReLU units;
- Adam optimizer;
- learning rate 0.0005;
- discount factor 0.99;
- epsilon decrement \(10^{-5}\);
- replay capacity \(10^6\);
- minibatch size 64;
- nominal training over 1,000 episodes/games.

There are two internal specification problems:

1. The text says the output layer has **three neurons**, even though the action table contains **four actions**.
2. The displayed target uses a direct maximum over the next-state network; it does not clearly show canonical DDQN's online-network argmax followed by target-network evaluation.

These are paper-level inconsistencies and must not be silently copied into code.

The training “game” ends when the success-rate counter reaches 100. One plot appears to extend to roughly 1,500 games even though the method description says 1,000, another minor reporting inconsistency.

### Evaluation and reported results

**Convergence**

- In low and medium congestion, the reward is reported to converge at about 600 games.
- Reliability rises from about 59% to about 80% during the first 400 games and ultimately approaches 95%.
- High-congestion training requires more than about 800 games.
- The redundant-action share rises from roughly 3% in low congestion to about 20% in high congestion.

**Approximate PRR read from the comparison plot**

| Method | Non-congested | Congested |
|---|---:|---:|
| Static hybrid duplication | ~0.99 | ~0.94 |
| DDQN | ~0.98 | ~0.90 |
| TOPSIS | ~0.89 | ~0.75 |
| ITS-G5 only | ~0.95 | ~0.66 |
| LTE-V2X only | ~0.54 | ~0.80 |

These values are plot readings, not tabulated exact numbers.

Under congestion, the text emphasizes that hybrid control increases PRR from about 66% for ITS-G5 alone to above 90%. The abstract summarizes improvements as up to 30 percentage points in PRR and about 20% more efficient use of redundant resources.

For 250 s of simulation, the static-hybrid policy creates 7,119 duplicate transmissions out of 10,718 transmissions, about 66%. The learned controller's duplicate share is visually close to 7% overall in the corresponding result, with scenario-specific values around 3–20%.

**Reviewer assessment:** the “20% resource efficiency” abstract phrase is not a transparent restatement of the much larger reduction from approximately 66% duplicate use to single-digit or low-double-digit use. Treat the reported duplicate counts and per-density action shares as the clearer evidence.

### URLLC treatment

The state includes application latency and reliability requirements, but:

- no numerical packet deadline is enforced as a terminal success condition;
- the paper assumes delay is below 100 ms;
- no finite-blocklength model or queue is present;
- no tail-latency statistic or reliability confidence bound is reported;
- no CMDP constraint is imposed.

This is application-aware soft-reward learning, not a formal URLLC-constrained controller.

### Main limitations

1. Straight highway, no intersections/buildings, no optical blockage, and a small five-car platoon.
2. Both links are RF and close in frequency.
3. LTE PC5 mode 3 does not match decentralized NR sidelink mode 2.
4. ACK/reception feedback is effectively an oracle unless its delay, loss, and airtime are modeled.
5. The reward is not fully specified.
6. Four actions conflict with a stated three-neuron output layer.
7. The displayed DDQN target is ambiguous.
8. Aggregate PRR remains far below stringent \(10^{-4}\)–\(10^{-5}\) loss targets.
9. No confidence intervals or rare-event methodology.

### Relationship to the planned implementation

**Direct overlap**

- A per-packet decision among single-link and duplicate-link actions.
- State variables such as SINR/SNIR, empirical PRR, link load, and application requirements.
- Explicit accounting for wasted duplicate traffic.
- Static duplication, single-link, and TOPSIS-like multi-criteria baselines.
- The practical distinction between decision-time, earliest feedback, and next-decision observations.

**Decisive gap**

- Dual RF rather than NR+VLC.
- Highway rather than Manhattan-grid mobility.
- Infrastructure-scheduled LTE rather than autonomous sidelink.
- Soft reward rather than hard reliability constraint.
- 100-ms timing assumption rather than a roughly 1-ms packet deadline.

### Coding implications

1. Represent observations and outcomes on a clear timeline: pre-action measurement, per-leg completion/ACK, deadline decision, and next pre-action observation.
2. Include rolling PRR/SINR and channel-busy/load estimates only if they could be measured at deployment time.
3. Implement `always RF`, `always VLC`, `always DUP`, threshold/TOPSIS, and unconstrained DQN/PPO baselines.
4. Track raw transmissions per successfully delivered packet and duplicate fraction, not only reward.
5. Do not include the “division” action unless the packet model is actually splittable; tiny deadline-critical safety packets normally make RF/VLC/DUP the cleaner set.
6. Verify that the policy output dimension exactly matches the action enumeration and implement the intended DDQN target explicitly if DDQN is used.
7. Model missing or delayed feedback rather than assuming instantaneous, lossless ACKs from every neighbor.

## W3 — Toward 6G-V2X: Aggregated RF-VLC for Ultra-Reliable and Low-Latency Autonomous Driving

### Exact record and access

Gurinder Singh, Anand Srivastava, Vivek Ashok Bohara, Md Noor-A-Rahim, Zilong Liu, and Dirk Pesch, “Toward 6G-V2X: Aggregated RF-VLC for Ultra-Reliable and Low-Latency Autonomous Driving,” *IEEE Communications Standards Magazine*, vol. 8, no. 4, pp. 80–87, December 2024. [DOI 10.1109/MCOMSTD.0001.2300005](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCOMSTD.0001.2300005).

**Full-text sources read:**

- [arXiv:2208.06287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06287), whose title uses “Towards”
- [Author-hosted published-version copy](https://narahim.github.io/assets/pdf/RF-VLC.pdf)

**Access depth: complete article**, with the final 2024 version used for citation and numerical reporting.

### Research question and contribution

This is a standards-oriented analytical/simulation article, not an RL paper. It argues that simultaneous aggregation of RF and VLC can combine:

- RF's longer range and resilience when the optical link weakens;
- VLC's high bandwidth, spatial reuse, and lower adjacent-lane interference;
- path diversity under interference and adverse weather.

The central comparison is:

- **link aggregation (LA):** transmit/use RF and VLC simultaneously;
- **non-link-aggregated hybrid:** select whichever single technology is preferable.

The numerical case study is a vehicle-to-infrastructure uplink at a road intersection, where a vehicle sends critical state such as position, speed, acceleration, and direction to an RSU mounted at the center of the intersection.

### System and channel model

#### Geometry and traffic

- The desired and interfering vehicles have both RF and VLC transceivers.
- Interferers occupy the same and perpendicular roads/lanes around an intersection.
- Vehicles are generated using a Poisson point process over a 10,000-m road extent.
- Interference from simultaneously transmitting vehicles is summed.
- Results average 10,000 point-process/fading realizations.
- Unless otherwise stated, vehicle-density and channel-access probability parameters are both reported as 0.01. The article does not make their physical units and all lane-wise interpretation sufficiently explicit for direct reconstruction.

#### VLC and RF

- VLC uses a Lambertian link model governed by irradiance angle, incidence angle/FoV, and transmitter–receiver distance.
- RF represents vehicular IEEE 802.11p.
- The discussion attributes VLC's reduced adjacent-lane interference partly to its restricted optical receiver FoV and longer short-range coherence compared with RF.
- At short range, the VLC leg is generally stronger; the paper's PRP curves indicate a crossover on the order of 120 m, beyond which RF becomes more useful. The exact crossover depends on weather and configuration.

#### Adverse-weather attenuation

The paper uses:

| Condition | Parameter | Attenuation coefficient |
|---|---|---:|
| rain | 90 mm/h | 21.9 dB/km |
| fog | visibility 0.05 km | 78.8 dB/km |
| dry snow | 10 mm/h | 131 dB/km |

These values are useful stress-test points, but should be verified against the underlying attenuation reference before being treated as a complete automotive VLC weather model.

### Reliability, latency, and rate analysis

#### Packet reception probability

Monte Carlo packet-reception probability is shown versus transmitter–RSU distance to 1 km. Pure RF and pure VLC have complementary distance regimes; LA is the strongest across the range because either leg can contribute.

#### Delay outage

The delay-outage rate is defined as the probability that the minimum transmission time for a payload exceeds a threshold:

\[
\mathrm{DOR}(T_{\mathrm{th}})
=\Pr\{T_{\min}>T_{\mathrm{th}}\}.
\]

The full transmission-time formula is delegated to an earlier paper rather than re-derived here. The article uses:

- a 50-KB payload;
- 20-MHz bandwidth for each pure RF and pure VLC link;
- distances of 50 m and 200 m;
- clear, rain, fog, and dry-snow cases.

Aggregation increases available bandwidth and lowers the plotted delay-outage probability relative to either single link.

#### Throughput

The LA throughput formula is likewise referenced to prior work. The displayed evaluation uses:

- link distances \(R\in\{50,100,150,200,250\}\) m;
- a desired-transmitter access probability near 0.9;
- aggregation overhead factor \(\beta_{\mathrm{ov}}=0.8\).

Under clear weather the paper reports an LA rate of 83.2 Mbit/s at 50 m and 39.8 Mbit/s at 250 m. The conclusion separately mentions roughly 78 Mbit/s at 50 m and 34 Mbit/s at 200 m under dry snow; these are different environmental points and should not be combined into one curve.

### What the “99.999% and <1 ms” statement establishes

**Paper claim:** the abstract and conclusion state that aggregated RF/VLC can achieve approximately 99.999% reliability and potentially less than 1-ms latency up to 200 m, including adverse conditions.

**Reviewer assessment:**

- The visible packet-reception plots near 200 m appear closer to ordinary high reliability (roughly the 0.9–0.99 range, depending on condition) than a directly demonstrated \(1-10^{-5}\) event probability.

- Ten thousand Monte Carlo realizations cannot resolve a \(10^{-5}\) probability with persuasive confidence unless special rare-event methods or analytical bounds are used; none are described.
- The key reliability and delay formulas are delegated to prior papers, and no confidence interval accompanies the claim.

Thus this article is strong **motivation and comparative physical insight**, but the five-nines statement should not be used as calibration evidence for a hard CMDP constraint.

### MDP/RL status

There is no MDP, state, action, reward, policy training, or learning baseline. LA is a fixed simultaneous-use architecture; non-LA is a conceptual single-link alternative. The future-work section itself calls for dynamic decisions between LA and non-LA as conditions, load, and user demand change.

### Main limitations

1. Stochastic but essentially static per-realization intersection geometry rather than a time-correlated mobility trace.
2. Weather and aggregate interference are modeled; explicit buildings, trucks, and third-vehicle optical occlusion are not.
3. No MAC scheduling or packet queue that jointly evolves with link choice.
4. No power/resource price for always aggregating.
5. No learned controller.
6. Critical equations are inherited from earlier references.
7. Ultra-high-reliability claims are not supported by rare-event sample sizes or confidence bounds in this article.

### Relationship to the planned implementation

**Direct overlap**

- RF/VLC diversity for safety messages.
- Simultaneous/duplicate use as a reliability mechanism.
- Intersection-centric motivation.
- Weather and directional-FoV effects.
- Evaluation by per-link and combined failure, delay outage, and resource overhead.

**Decisive gap**

- No blockage state or constrained learning.

### Coding implications

1. Use fixed LA/always-DUP as an upper-reliability, high-resource baseline.
2. Include weather coefficients as named, configurable stress cases, with their provenance documented.
3. Keep per-leg success correlated through shared geometry/weather rather than multiplying marginal reliabilities blindly.

## W4 — Evaluation of a New Radio Technology and Visible Light Communication for a Platooning Application

### Exact record and access

Fouzi Boukhalfa, Mohamed Hadded, Paul Mühlethaler, and Oyunchimeg Shagdar, “Evaluation of a New Radio Technology and Visible Light Communication for a Platooning Application,” *2021 International Symposium on Networks, Computers and Communications (ISNCC)*, pp. 1–7. [DOI 10.1109/ISNCC52172.2021.9615646](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISNCC52172.2021.9615646).

**Full text read:** [HAL record hal-03364383](https://hal.science/hal-03364383v1) and [open manuscript](https://hal.science/hal-03364383/document). **Access depth: complete conference paper.**

### Research question and contribution

The paper evaluates whether:

1. a distributed TDMA-like RF design called AS-DTMAC can meet platooning update requirements; and
2. V-VLC simulated with empirically measured headlamp radiation can supplement RF under high load.

It discusses four architectural modes—VLC, RF, redundant RF+VLC, or one selected technology—but does **not** build or evaluate an adaptive hybrid selector. The RF and VLC portions are largely separate analyses.

The target platooning requirements quoted by the paper are:

- beacon/update frequency: 10 Hz;
- recommended latency: 20 ms;
- reliability: 99.99%.

These are requirements motivating the work, not end-to-end results proven by a single hybrid system.

### RF: AS-DTMAC analysis

AS-DTMAC divides the road into geographic zones with local time slots. When a vehicle changes zones it loses its old slot and reserves a new one through active signaling. For a platoon, all members must obtain slots in less than the 100-ms update interval.

Let \(n\) be the number of platoon vehicles, and let each candidate slot be occupied independently with probability \(\lambda\). If \(I_j\) is the number of occupied slots skipped before vehicle \(j\) obtains a free slot, then:

\[
\Pr(I_1=i_1,\ldots,I_n=i_n)
=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda)\lambda^{i_j},
\]

\[
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1+I_j)\right]
=\frac{n}{1-\lambda},
\]

and the total-extra-slot distribution is negative-binomial:

\[
\Pr\!\left(n+\sum_j I_j=n+p\right)
=\binom{n+p-1}{n-1}\lambda^p(1-\lambda)^n.
\]

The paper uses an approximately 33-slot frame and studies a platoon of up to ten vehicles. Except at high load, its model predicts that all ten reserve within the frame.

For connectivity it uses:

\[
PL(d)=10\gamma\log(d/d_0)+PL_0(d_0)+X,
\]

where \(X\) is a zero-mean random fading term with standard deviation \(\sigma\), and varies \(\gamma\) approximately from 2.2 to 3. The physical layer is based on IEEE 802.11p at 5.9 GHz with \(-85\) dBm sensitivity for 64-QAM 3/4. The Monte Carlo result supports “safe connectivity” to about 150 m under the selected assumptions.

**Reviewer assessment:** the independent slot-occupancy approximation and the claim that greedy active signaling has negligible collisions are capacity approximations, not a full network MAC simulation with hidden nodes and correlated platoon arrivals.

### VLC simulation

The Veins-VLC model uses measured HELLA automotive headlight patterns, including high-/low-beam asymmetry and a non-line-of-sight ground component. The measurement setup used a real pair of headlamp modules and two PDA100A photodiodes.

Main simulation parameters:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| packet size | 1,024 bytes |
| beacon frequency | 10 Hz |
| rate | 1 Mbit/s |
| car following | CACC |
| platoon size in interference study | 8 |
| platoon spacing | 10 m by default |
| platoon speed | 15 km/h |
| disturbing-vehicle speed | 50–120 km/h |

The road has a platoon lane and adjacent traffic whose headlights interfere while passing.

### Reported VLC/RF findings

**VLC interference**

- PDR falls as the number of disturbing vehicles grows.
- With 100 disturbing vehicles, about 40% of VLC packets are lost in the representative result.
- Different commercial headlamp types create as much as roughly a 10-percentage-point PDR spread at high load.
- Faster passing traffic improves PDR because each interferer remains alongside the platoon for less time.
- Increasing platoon spacing from 10 to 40 m sharply lowers PDR; approximate readings for 40/70/100 interferers fall from about 80/72/58% near 10 m to about 56/41/15% near 40 m.

Those latter values are approximate plot readings, not source tables.

**Latency**

- VLC delay rises from about 1 to 9 ms when packet size rises from 100 to 1,200 bytes.
- Multi-hop forwarding from a remote platoon member adds latency, so platoon length matters.

**RF comparison**

- At the largest shown disturbance level, RF loses less than about 3% of packets.
- VLC is around the 60–65% PDR range under that heavy optical-interference case.
- RF is more robust in this particular scenario; VLC is positioned as an assisting, spatially reusable technology whose performance is sensitive to spacing and interfering headlights.

### Hybrid-control and URLLC status

The article illustrates:

- VLC-only operation;
- RF-only operation;
- redundant RF+VLC operation;
- selecting one technology at a time.

However, it does not specify a state machine, threshold policy, MDP, learned policy, or measured performance for an adaptive selector. Its conclusion suggests future smart switching based on CBR/CAM load, with redundancy used when load permits.

The paper quotes 99.99% and 20 ms as platooning requirements. The joint RF/VLC system is not evaluated against an explicit deadline-miss probability or reliability constraint, and no finite-blocklength/queue model is present.

### Main limitations

1. Separate RF analysis and VLC simulation rather than an integrated hybrid experiment.
2. No learned or optimized RF/VLC controller.
3. Highway/platoon topology, not Manhattan intersections.
4. Optical impairment is interfering headlights; explicit third-vehicle blockage is absent.
5. Simplified independent slot occupancy and assumed negligible reservation collisions.
6. No confidence intervals or rare-event test for 99.99%.
7. IEEE 802.11p-based RF rather than NR sidelink.

### Relationship to the planned implementation

**Direct overlap**

- Real automotive beam-pattern evidence.
- 10-Hz, 1,024-byte safety-message reference point.
- RF, VLC, and redundant modes.
- Network load/CBR as an observable for choosing a technology.
- Switch count, PDR, optical-interference duration, and spacing sensitivity.

**Decisive gap**

- No actual joint controller and no CMDP.
- Highway platoon rather than urban free-moving vehicles.
- No hard 1-ms packet deadline and no third-vehicle occlusion.

### Coding implications

1. Add CBR/load-threshold and simple hysteresis baselines inspired by the paper's future design.
2. Validate VLC PDR trends against spacing, relative speed, and empirical headlamp family.
3. Separate optical co-channel interference from physical line-of-sight blockage in the environment.
4. Use the 1–9-ms serialization/delay range as a warning: a 1-ms deadline cannot be claimed with 1-Mbit/s VLC and kilobyte packets without changing packet size, rate, coding, or deadline definition.
5. Add an RF-capacity/load sanity check rather than modeling RF as an unlimited reliable fallback.
6. Do not cite W4 as having solved adaptive hybrid switching; it only motivates it.

## W6 — Design and Optimization of a Hybrid VLC/THz Infrastructure-to-Vehicle Communication System for Intelligent Transportation

### Exact record and access

Yusef Modami, Hamzeh Beiranvand, and Mohammad Taghi Dabiri, “Design and Optimization of a Hybrid VLC/THz Infrastructure-to-Vehicle Communication System for Intelligent Transportation,” arXiv preprint, submitted 19 August 2025. [arXiv:2508.14225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14225), [DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2508.14225](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.14225).

**Version caveat:** this is a preprint, not a peer-reviewed final record found during the search. The arXiv metadata spells the second author's surname “Beiranvand,” while one line in the PDF spells it “Beyranvad”; the metadata spelling is used here. **Access depth: complete 13-page preprint.**

### Research question and contribution

The paper designs a streetlight-based infrastructure-to-vehicle access network combining:

- visible-light downlink from LED streetlights;
- a 144-GHz THz antenna-array backup;
- a previously proposed hybrid FSO/THz backhaul;
- physical transceiver placement/orientation optimization;
- a proposed switching-combining (PSC) mechanism.

It optimizes **spatial coverage maps**, not a packet policy under moving traffic. Its value for the planned project is primarily physical modeling, geometry, weather stress tests, and the idea of hysteretic switch/combine control.

### Geometry and infrastructure

The access-layer evaluation uses:

- \(K=3\) streetlights;
- a two-way road, 10 m wide per direction;
- pole height 10 m;
- vehicle height 1.5 m;
- default pole spacing 30 m, with optimized/custom design extending this to 50 m;
- two VLC/THz receivers positioned on the vehicle roof;
- two lamps/transmitters per pole with configurable mounting and orientation angles.

The road is discretized into spatial grid points. Regions 0–1 m and 9–10 m across a lane are excluded because a vehicle center cannot occupy the borders.

The proposed physical design increases the number of tunable beam/receiver angles, separates roof receivers, and offsets poles on opposite sides to improve desired coverage and reduce cross-road interference.

### VLC model

For a transmitter/receiver pair \(i,k\), the received contribution is:

\[
P_{ik}=
\frac{P_t A_R T_s g(m+1)h_{aa}
\cos^m(\phi_{S,ik})\cos(\phi_{R,ik})}
{2\pi L_{ik}^2},
\]

when transmitter and receiver angles lie inside their FoVs, and zero otherwise. Total received VLC power is the sum over the two lamps and two roof receivers:

\[
P_c=\sum_{k,i\in\{-1,1\}}P_{ik}.
\]

Atmospheric loss is Beer–Lambert:

\[
h_{aa}=e^{-\alpha_aL},\qquad
\alpha_a=\frac{3.912}{V_i}
\left(\frac{\lambda_V}{550}\right)^{-\delta(V_i)},
\]

with the visibility-dependent exponent:

| visibility \(V_i\) | \(\delta(V_i)\) |
|---|---:|
| \(>50\) km | 1.6 |
| 6–50 km | 1.3 |
| 1–6 km | \(0.16V_i+0.34\) |
| 0.5–1 km | \(V_i-0.5\) |
| \(<0.5\) km | 0 |

The VLC electrical SNR and SINR are:

\[
\mathrm{SNR}_{V}=\frac{(\eta P_c)^2}{\sigma_{nV}^2},
\]

\[
\mathrm{SINR}_{V}
=\frac{(\eta P_c)^2}
{\sigma_{nV}^2+\left[\eta(P_{bs}+P'_c)\right]^2},
\]

where noise includes shot and thermal terms, \(P_{bs}\) is background-light power, and \(P'_c\) is opposite-side streetlight interference.

### THz model

The THz leg uses planar arrays with normalized array factors, half-wavelength element spacing, free-space loss, and frequency-selective molecular absorption. The received power sums the two transmitter–receiver contributions:

\[
P_{\mathrm{THz}}=\sum_{k,i}P_{\mathrm{THz},ik}.
\]

The path coefficient is modeled as a product of free-space and molecular-loss factors; six principal absorption lines are included. Small-scale fading and misalignment are explicitly neglected because the link is only tens of meters and the study seeks deterministic street-surface coverage.

The THz SNR/SINR take the conventional received-power-over-noise/interference form. This makes the result a best-aligned physical-design study rather than a robust mobile-beam-tracking study.

## W7 — Deep Reinforcement Learning-Based Adaptive Handover Mechanism for VLC in a Hybrid 6G Network Architecture

### Exact record and access

Liqiang Wang, Dahai Han, Min Zhang, Danshi Wang, and Zhiguo Zhang, “Deep Reinforcement Learning-Based Adaptive Handover Mechanism for VLC in a Hybrid 6G Network Architecture,” *IEEE Access*, vol. 9, pp. 87241–87250, 2021. [DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3089521](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3089521).

**Full text read:** the [public CC BY 4.0 accepted manuscript on ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352418995_Deep_Reinforcement_Learning-Based_Adaptive_Handover_Mechanism_for_VLC_in_a_Hybrid_6G_Network_Architecture), with metadata cross-checked through the [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/3a2936e7167a4dd181f47b0fe8f3fd82). **Access depth: complete accepted manuscript.**

### Research question and actual scope

This paper tackles frequent handovers among ultra-dense indoor VLC access points as a user device moves through overlapping optical cells. A 6G indoor RF base station/hotspot acts as the coordinator and potential connectivity fallback.

Despite the “hybrid” title, the learned action is **which VLC AP to use**, not whether to use RF, VLC, or both. RF provides coordination/control and architectural fallback. Accordingly, W7 is a handover-policy precedent, not a direct RAT-selection precedent for the planned action space.

Its two main proposals are:

1. a make-before-break handover protocol with sequence-number synchronization; and
2. a DQN variant whose replay-memory size decreases during training.

### System and channel model

#### Architecture and protocol

- A central indoor 6G hotspot/BS controls densely deployed VLC APs.
- Users periodically report received signal strengths from visible APs.
- The coordinator infers the nearest/candidate AP and runs AP selection.
- It sends a handover request to the target AP and obtains an ACK.
- It requests the user's sequence-number status and forwards that status to the target AP.
- The original AP continues transmitting during negotiation.
- The target becomes current only after the user reports a successful new connection.

This is effectively make-before-break with data-sequence synchronization, designed to prevent interruption or retransmission ambiguity at handover.

#### VLC rate

If \(u_{i,t}\) users share AP \(i\) in TDD mode, the per-user rate is modeled as:

\[
R_m=\frac{B}{u_{i,t}}\log_2(1+\Gamma).
\]

The optical SINR uses squared received photocurrent from transmit power \(P_{tr}\), channel gain \(h_c\), and detector response \(R_{pd}\), divided by bandwidth-scaled noise plus interference. The LOS Lambertian gain is:

\[
h_c=
\frac{(m+1)A_{da}}{2\pi w_{u,v}^{2}}
\cos^m(\phi)\,g_s(\vartheta)\,g(\vartheta)\cos(\vartheta),
\]

inside the receiver FoV, with the standard ideal concentrator gain \(g(\vartheta)=n^2/\sin^2\Psi_M\); it is zero outside the FoV.

The authors say the mechanism could apply to NLOS but model **LOS only**.

### RL formulation

#### Agent, state, and action

- Each user device is described as an agent, although the decision computation is centralized.
- Movement changes received signal strengths, producing the state feedback.
- RSS reports are also converted to AP-location information and used as training input.
- The paper does not give a precise state vector, dimension, normalization, or history window.
- The action is one of the currently visible VLC APs.
- A user can see at most four APs because of the assumed regular deployment and overlap geometry, so \(0<N\le4\) candidate actions.

This formulation has a variable candidate set, but the paper does not explain padding/masking or the neural output representation when \(N\) changes.

#### Reward

The reward is an AP-distance-shaped quantity. The paper assumes a relationship between user–AP distance and reward based on a cumulative Poisson distribution:

\[
R=F_{\mathrm{Poisson}}(r-b_mr_i;\lambda,a_m,\ldots),
\]

in the notation of its equation (7), where \(r\) is AP radius, \(r_i\) is user–AP distance, \(\lambda\) is an average incidence parameter, and \(a_m,b_m\) are correlation coefficients.

The PDF equation is typeset ambiguously, and the numerical coefficients are not disclosed. It is therefore not possible to reconstruct the reward exactly from this paper alone.

#### Modified DQN

The proposed modification starts with a large replay memory for broad early exploration, then monotonically shrinks it so late training focuses on recent experience. Two desired mappings are stated:

\[
E_r=a_eE_p+b_e
\]

between replay size \(E_r\) and episode \(E_p\), and

\[
E_r=a_r\log(b_rR+c_r)+d_r
\]

between replay size and reward.

All coefficients are left unspecified, and the paper does not explain how the two mappings are reconciled when they imply different sizes.

Other disclosed learning details:

- epsilon-greedy action selection;
- comparison at \(\epsilon\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}\);
- target and evaluation Q estimates and experience replay in the general DQN description;
- 1,000 training episodes.

Not disclosed:

- neural input/output dimensions and hidden layers;
- learning rate, optimizer, discount factor used in the DQN experiment;
- batch size, initial/final memory capacities, target-update frequency;
- replay-shrink coefficients;
- training/evaluation seed count.

The manuscript calls the DQN approximator a deep convolutional neural network, but no spatial tensor or convolutional architecture is specified.

### Simulation parameters and evaluation

The optical parameters are:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| bandwidth per AP | 100 MHz |
| AP optical transmit power | 3 W |
| detector responsivity | 0.53 A/W |
| detector-noise PSD | \(10^{-21}\) A²/Hz |
| photodetector area | \(10^{-4}\) m² |
| LED half-intensity angle | \(30^\circ\) |
| receiver FoV semi-angle | \(60^\circ\) |
| assumed transmission delay | 0.7 ms |
| effective AP coverage radius | 2 m |

The user follows one fixed path. Depending on the selected AP at every overlap, it could switch at most 46 times and at least four times. Results use movement speeds over roughly 0.5–1.5 m/s and compare:

- proposed decreasing-replay DQN;
- ordinary DQN;
- tabular Sarsa;
- tabular Q-learning;
- trajectory-based handover skipping (HS), for the handover-count comparison.

### Reported results

At 0.5 m/s, the proposed method is reported to reduce handovers relative to:

| Baseline | Reduction in handover count |
|---|---:|
| HS | 66% |
| Q-learning | 55% |
| Sarsa | 52% |
| ordinary DQN | 42% |

At higher speed, Q-learning and Sarsa degrade enough that HS can outperform them, while DQN and the proposed method remain better. The proposed policy approaches the theoretical four-handover minimum, with residual oscillation attributed to epsilon-greedy exploration.

For average downlink rate under the authors' best-convergence comparison:

- +8% over ordinary DQN;
- +13% over Sarsa;
- +13% over Q-learning.

At the high exploration rate \(\epsilon=0.4\), the maximum displayed advantage over traditional RL is reported as 48%.

**Reviewer assessment**

- No raw rate table or uncertainty accompanies the percentage gains.
- Training and evaluation use a fixed path, so the results do not show generalization to new layouts or paths.
- The claimed mechanism is not reproducible from the disclosed replay schedule, reward, and architecture.
- A replay buffer that shrinks toward only recent data can help a nonstationary problem, but in a stationary training environment it can also increase correlation and catastrophic forgetting. The paper does not ablate those effects.
- Reducing handovers and increasing rate are related through assumed overhead, but the reward itself is a distance-based Poisson shape rather than a transparent measured-rate-minus-handover-cost objective.

### URLLC status

The architecture is motivated by low latency, continuity, and reliability. The protocol avoids breaking the old link before the new one is ready, and Table I assumes 0.7-ms transmission delay. Nevertheless:

- handover signaling/processing is not simulated packet by packet;
- no loss probability, deadline distribution, queue, or finite-blocklength model is given;
- there is no reliability constraint;
- 0.7 ms is an assumed component value, not a demonstrated end-to-end deadline.

W7 is not a URLLC-constrained evaluation.

### Main limitations

1. Indoor low-speed user, not vehicular V2V.
2. Learned AP selection within VLC, not RF/VLC RAT selection.
3. LOS only, no human/vehicle blockage in the experiment.
4. Fixed path and no held-out layout.
5. State, reward coefficients, neural architecture, and replay schedule are incompletely specified.
6. Variable candidate-action handling is unexplained.
7. No hard reliability or measured handover-delay outcome.
8. Percentage improvements without uncertainty or raw rate data.

### Relationship to the planned implementation

**Useful overlap**

- Ping-pong/switching is a real cost.
- A make-before-break procedure resembles temporary duplicate use while state transfers.
- RSS history and current candidate availability can inform decisions.
- Handover count and service continuity should be evaluated separately.

**Decisive gap**

- AP-to-AP horizontal optical handover rather than NR/VLC vertical RAT selection.
- Indoor pedestrian speed rather than vehicles on a grid.
- Central control and sequence synchronization rather than decentralized sidelink.
- No packet reliability CMDP.

### Coding implications

1. Preserve the current link until the selected replacement has completed, if the simulator models multi-step handover; otherwise explicitly account for make-before-break resource use.
2. Add action hysteresis or switch cost and report ping-pong rate.
3. Keep action masks explicit when a link/AP is unavailable.
4. Do not copy the decreasing replay idea without an ablation against fixed replay, recency-weighted replay, and recurrent state.
5. Specify every reward coefficient and replay/network hyperparameter in configuration.
6. Evaluate on held-out routes and layouts rather than one fixed path.

## W8 — DQN-Based Multi-User Power Allocation for Hybrid RF/VLC Networks

### Exact record and access

Bekir Sait Ciftler, Mohamed Abdallah, Abdulmalik Alwarafy, and Mounir Hamdi, “DQN-Based Multi-User Power Allocation for Hybrid RF/VLC Networks,” *ICC 2021—IEEE International Conference on Communications*, pp. 1–6. [DOI 10.1109/ICC42927.2021.9500564](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICC42927.2021.9500564).

**Full-text sources read:**

- [arXiv:2102.01884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01884)
- [HKUST institutional record](https://repository.hkust.edu.hk/ir/Record/1783.1-163883)

**Version caveat:** the arXiv manuscript orders Alwarafy before Abdallah; the indexed final conference citation orders Abdallah before Alwarafy. Use the final record's order in the bibliography. **Access depth: complete conference manuscript.**

### Research question and contribution

W8 studies transmit-power allocation in an indoor downlink containing one RF AP, four VLC APs, and multihomed users. Every AP is an independent DQN agent. Each chooses a discrete vector of per-user power allocations so that total RF+VLC user rate stays close to a target while obeying the AP power budget.

It contributes:

1. a continuous observation for DQN, contrasted with a coarse three-level tabular-Q state;
2. a common reward penalizing absolute target-rate error;
3. a convergence comparison between independent multi-agent DQN and tabular Q-learning.

This is simultaneous **rate aggregation and power control**, not duplicate packet delivery or RAT selection.

### System and channel model

#### Topology

- 12 m × 12 m indoor room, ceiling height 3 m.
- RF AP at the room-center origin.
- Four VLC APs at \((-3,-3),(-3,3),(3,-3),(3,3)\).
- Two users placed uniformly at random in each Monte Carlo experiment.
- Users have simultaneous RF and VLC multihoming.
- VLC coverage regions are treated as exclusive; VLC APs use orthogonal frequencies.
- Each AP divides its bandwidth equally among users in its coverage.

#### VLC

The LOS optical gain follows:

\[
G_{\mathrm{VLC}}^{(u,l)}(t)=
\frac{(m+1)A_{pd}\lambda
\cos^m\theta_{tx}^{(u,l)}(t)}
{2\pi[x^{(u,l)}(t)^2+y^2]}
H_f(\theta_{rx})H_c(\theta_{rx})\cos\theta_{rx},
\]

with vertically upward user photodiodes and a conventional FoV-limited concentrator.

The achievable VLC rate is:

\[
R_{\mathrm{VLC}}^{(u,l)}(t)
=\frac{W_{\mathrm{VLC}}}{2}
\log_2\!\left[
1+
\frac{\left(\kappa m_dP_{\mathrm{VLC}}^{(u,l)}(t)
G_{\mathrm{VLC}}^{(u,l)}(t)\right)^2}
{W_{\mathrm{VLC}}\sigma_{\mathrm{VLC}}^2}
\right].
\]

#### RF

RF channel gain is:

\[
G_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t)
=10^{-L(d_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t))/10}
|h_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t)|^2,
\]

\[
L(d)=47.9+10\nu\log_{10}(d/d_0)+X,
\]

with \(d_0=1\) m, path-loss exponent \(\nu=1.6\), zero-mean shadowing of 1.8-dB standard deviation, and an exponential small-scale-fading variable whose mean is reported as 2.46 dB.

RF rate is:

\[
R_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t)
=W_{\mathrm{RF}}\log_2\left(
1+\frac{P_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t)G_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t)}
{W_{\mathrm{RF}}\sigma_{\mathrm{RF}}^2}
\right).
\]

The multihomed user's rate is the **sum**:

\[
R^{(u)}(t)=R_{\mathrm{RF}}^{(u)}(t)+R_{\mathrm{VLC}}^{(u,l)}(t).
\]

This sum-rate semantics is fundamentally different from duplicate safety packets, whose success is the union of two delivery events and whose payload rate is not additive.

### Optimization, state, action, and reward

#### Utility and target band

For target rate \(T^{(u)}\), the acceptable band is:

\[
B^{(u)}=\max\{0.05T^{(u)},0.5\}.
\]

The per-step common utility/reward is:

\[
r_t=\sum_{u=1}^{N}
\left[B^{(u)}-\left|R^{(u)}(t)-T^{(u)}\right|\right].
\]

It rewards being near the target and penalizes both under- and over-provision. The optimization imposes nonnegative per-user allocations and a maximum total power for every AP.

#### Q-learning state

For each user, the tabular state is:

\[
s_t^{(u)}=
\begin{cases}
1,&R^{(u)}(t)<T^{(u)},\\
2,&R^{(u)}(t)>T^{(u)}+B^{(u)},\\
3,&T^{(u)}\le R^{(u)}(t)\le T^{(u)}+B^{(u)}.
\end{cases}
\]

The system state concatenates all user entries.

#### DQN state

The DQN observation preserves continuous values:

\[
s_t^{(u)}=[R^{(u)}(t),T^{(u)}]^\top,
\]

concatenated across users. Each AP sees the same rate/target state and knows which users are in its coverage.

#### Actions

Each VLC AP selects a vector:

\[
a_i=[a_i^{(1)},\ldots,a_i^{(U)}],
\quad a_i^{(u)}\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{VLC}},
\quad \sum_u a_i^{(u)}\le P_{\mathrm{VLC}}^{\max}.
\]

The RF AP uses the analogous vector over \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{RF}}\). Thus the action is discrete but combinatorial. The paper does not disclose the exact power-level sets or the resulting action counts \(V_A,R_A\).

### DQN/QL details

- One independent agent for each of four VLC APs and the RF AP.
- No direct inter-agent communication.
- All agents receive the same global reward.
- Epsilon schedule:

\[
\epsilon(t)=\max\{0.99^{t-1},0.1\}.
\]

- Reported learning-rate parameter \(\alpha=0.5\).
- Discount factor \(\gamma=0.5\).
- Each DQN has three hidden layers of 32 nodes.
- Mean-squared-error loss and Adam optimizer.
- Separate replay memory for each AP.

**Reviewer assessment and missing implementation details**

- A common payoff makes this a cooperative/common-interest learning problem with decentralized learners, despite the paper calling it “non-cooperative.”
- Algorithm 2 uses the same network for the current estimate and \(\max Q(s',a)\); no separate target network is shown. This is closer to replay-based neural Q-learning than a fully specified stabilized DQN.
- Replay capacity \(M\), minibatch size, target-update rule, activation functions, exact optimizer learning rate, and power discretization are not disclosed.
- It is unclear whether the table's \(\alpha=0.5\) is intended as Adam's learning rate; if so, it is unusually large.
- Independent learners see a nonstationary environment because all AP policies change simultaneously.

### Simulation parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| RF maximum transmit power | 0.01 W |
| RF noise PSD | \(-57\) dBm/MHz |
| RF bandwidth | 5 MHz |
| VLC maximum transmit power | 2 W |
| VLC noise PSD | \(-100\) dBm/MHz |
| VLC bandwidth per AP | 20 MHz |
| PD FoV half-angle | \(45^\circ\) |
| LED half-power angle | \(60^\circ\) |
| PD area | \(10^{-4}\) m² |
| PD responsivity | 0.4 |
| optical-filter gain | 1 |
| concentrator refractive index | 1.5 |
| optical/electrical efficiency | 1 |
| VLC APs / RF APs / users | 4 / 1 / 2 |

Users are randomized between Monte Carlo trials rather than following a disclosed mobility process within an episode.

### Evaluation and reported results

Convergence is defined as **all users' average rates remaining inside their target bands for at least 100 iterations**.

For a sample with targets 20 and 12 Mbit/s:

- DQN reaches the desired joint state in 306 iterations;
- Q-learning reaches individual targets at different times but requires roughly 1,900–2,000 iterations to satisfy both simultaneously.

Across 1,000 Monte Carlo experiments:

| Metric | DQN | Q-learning |
|---|---:|---:|
| median convergence time | 203 iterations | 1,989 iterations |
| fraction that converges | 96.1% | 72.3% |

The median improvement is about 89.8%, reported as 90% shorter. The DQN solutions also lie closer to the exact targets because their continuous state retains rate error that the three-bin Q table discards.

**Reviewer assessment**

- This is primarily evidence that coarse state quantization harms a tabular baseline; it is not evidence that DQN is optimal relative to model-based optimization, actor-critic methods, centralized multi-agent training, or continuous power control.
- “Convergence time” counts algorithm iterations in a static resource-allocation loop, not wall-clock learning or online vehicle-control latency.
- No achieved power-consumption table, generalization test, uncertainty beyond the empirical CDF, or nonstationary-user experiment is given.

### URLLC status

QoS is a target average data rate. There is no:

- packet error model;
- packet deadline;
- queue or arrival process;
- reliability/tail constraint;
- finite-blocklength calculation;
- vehicular mobility.

W8 does not evaluate URLLC, despite being useful for hybrid resource-control mathematics.

### Main limitations

1. Indoor infrastructure network, not V2V.
2. Static random user placement and no blocker dynamics.
3. Sum-rate aggregation, not redundant packet delivery.
4. Discrete power vectors can grow combinatorially with users and levels.
5. Key DQN details and power levels are missing.
6. No stabilized target network is specified.
7. Independent common-reward agents create nonstationarity without coordination analysis.
8. Only tabular Q-learning is a substantive algorithmic baseline.
9. No hard reliability or latency objective.

### Relationship to the planned implementation

**Useful overlap**

- Hybrid RF/VLC channel modularization.
- Explicit power budgets and resource cost.
- Continuous observations are preferable to arbitrary bins.
- A target band can avoid oscillatory over-correction.
- The paper shows how joint RF/VLC resource allocation differs from simple link selection.

**Decisive gap**

- Indoor AP power vectors rather than per-packet V2V actions.
- Rate addition rather than delivery diversity.
- No mobility, NR sidelink, VLC blockage, or CMDP.

### Coding implications

1. Do not use W8's sum-rate equation for duplicate packets; implement success as `RF_success OR VLC_success` by the deadline.
2. If power is fixed in the planned scope, keep power allocation outside the RL action and use W8 only to structure channel/resource modules.
3. If power becomes controllable later, avoid enumerating all per-user vectors; use a continuous or factorized policy with projection onto the power budget.
4. Prefer continuous normalized observations over coarse quality bins.
5. Report power/transmission resource alongside reliability.
6. If a neural Q baseline is implemented, use a target network, double-Q target, disclosed replay/batch settings, and multiple seeds.

## Cross-paper synthesis for the planned coding phase

### What is genuinely established by these eight works

1. **RF and VLC are physically complementary.** W3, W4, W5, and W6 consistently show a short-range/LOS/spatial-reuse advantage for optical links and a coverage/blockage/load fallback role for RF or THz.
2. **Always duplicating is reliable but expensive.** W2 quantifies the traffic waste; W3 treats aggregation as a performance upper bound; W1 learns to use duplication rarely in an easy two-car scenario.
3. **Switching itself matters.** W1 and W7 quantify switch/handover count; W6 uses upper/lower thresholds; W4 explicitly proposes load-aware switching as future work.
4. **Observable link state matters.** W2 uses SNIR/PRR/application demand; W7 uses RSS and location; W8 shows that retaining continuous error beats coarse bins. W1's geometry-only state is acknowledged by its own authors as incomplete.
5. **Real vehicle-light patterns matter.** W1 and W4 use measured HELLA beam patterns and show asymmetry/headlamp-family sensitivity.
6. **The strongest “URLLC” language is not matched by a hard sequential reliability formulation.** Across W1–W8 there is no rare-event-validated, packet-deadline CMDP.

### What remains open—and is the planned work's defensible novelty

| Needed element | W1 | W2 | W3 | W4 | W5 | W6 | W7 | W8 |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| RF/VLC vehicular decision | ✓ | — | fixed LA | discussed | rule | — | — | — |
| NR sidelink | — | — | — | — | mentions only | — | — | — |
| Manhattan-grid mobility | — | — | intersection PPP only | — | — | — | — | — |
| explicit third-vehicle VLC blockage | — | — | — | — | — | — | — | — |
| RF/VLC/DUP per packet | ✓ | dual-RF | fixed DUP | discussed | — | — | — | sum rate |
| deadline-defined packet success | — | — | delay outage | partial delay | — | — | — | — |
| hard reliability CMDP | — | — | — | — | — | — | — | — |
| measurable partially observed state/history | — | partial | — | — | partial rule | — | partial | continuous but static |
| rare-event evaluation/confidence | — | — | — | — | — | — | — | — |

The combination of all rows is not present in W1–W8.

### Recommended environment decomposition

The literature supports keeping the simulator modular:

1. **Mobility/geometry**
   - Manhattan lanes, turns, headings, inter-vehicle geometry.
   - Desired transmitter, receiver, and third-vehicle blockers.
2. **NR sidelink RF**
   - path loss, LOS/NLOS/building state, fading, interference/load, scheduling/access delay, packet decoding.
3. **V-VLC**
   - empirical or validated asymmetric beam;
   - irradiance/incidence/FoV;
   - distance and weather attenuation;
   - blocking intersection with third vehicles;
   - ambient and vehicle-light interference;
   - optical transmission time.
4. **Traffic and deadline**
   - packet arrivals and size;
   - per-leg completion time and success;
   - combined DUP outcome;
   - late packets counted as failures.
5. **Controller observation**
   - only deployable measurements: recent RF/VLC SINR or CQI, rolling ACK/PRR, CBR/load, relative kinematics if sensors provide them, prior action, time since switch, and recent blockage cues.
6. **CMDP signals**
   - reward/objective for resource use, energy, or duplication;
   - separate cost \(c_t=\mathbf 1\{\text{packet missed deadline}\}\);
   - explicit constraint budget and Lagrange/dual state.

### Baseline set justified by W1–W8

At minimum:

1. always RF;
2. always VLC when geometrically available, with a documented fallback for unavailable VLC;
3. always DUP/LA;
4. oracle best single leg using future per-packet outcomes, labeled as an unattainable upper bound;
5. fixed SINR/PRR or CBR threshold;
6. two-threshold hysteresis/TTT policy;
7. W5-style QoS rule, fully specified;
8. TOPSIS or weighted multi-criteria policy;
9. unconstrained PPO or DQN using the same observation/action space;
10. the proposed constrained method.

If W1 is claimed as reproduced, its eight actions, position-angle state, and undocumented cost issue must be handled transparently; otherwise label the implementation “W1-inspired PPO,” not an exact reproduction.

### Evaluation protocol the prior papers leave missing

1. Use disjoint training, validation, and test maps/seeds.
2. Test held-out traffic densities, turn probabilities, blocker sizes, weather, RF load, and sensor noise.
3. Report:
   - deadline delivery probability and miss probability;
   - one-sided confidence bound on miss probability;
   - mean and tail latency;
   - RF/VLC/DUP action shares;
   - transmissions, airtime, and energy per delivered packet;
   - switch count and ping-pong count;
   - constraint violation during training and evaluation;
   - conditional failure by blockage/intersection/density regime.
4. Use enough test packets to support the claimed reliability order. Zero failures in a small test is not proof of five nines.
5. Run multiple training seeds and show central tendency plus dispersion.
6. Compare at matched reliability, not only matched scalar reward.
7. Audit observation leakage: the policy must not see future blockage, realized packet success, or exact latent channel variables unavailable to a deployed controller.

### Reproducibility traps identified before coding

- W1 omits numerical action costs/success-probability construction.
- W2 has four actions but states a three-neuron output and incompletely specifies reward terms.
- W3 delegates core delay/rate expressions and does not statistically establish its five-nines headline.
- W4 does not actually evaluate hybrid switching.
- W5 gives no simulator/channel configuration.
- W6 delegates PSC conditions and optimizes/evaluates on one spatial layout.
- W7 omits state/network/replay/reward coefficients.
- W8 omits power levels, replay/batch/target-network details and uses rate aggregation rather than duplication.

These gaps should be called out in the literature review and should motivate a configuration-complete, seed-controlled codebase.
